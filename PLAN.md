## Competitor Teardowns (5-7)
1. **The Spruce Pets (Pets Section)**
   - *Block Order*: Hero (Featured Article) -> Latest Articles -> Category Grid -> Newsletter.
   - *Gaps*: Very broad, treats are a tiny sub-category. No calculators. Stock photos heavily used.
2. **Rover.com (Blog)**
   - *Block Order*: Search -> Trending -> Product Reviews -> Advice.
   - *Gaps*: Often written by generalists, lacks primary source vet citations on snack digestion.
3. **DogFoodAdvisor**
   - *Block Order*: Warning list -> Latest Reviews -> Search.
   - *Gaps*: Excellent on kibble/wet food, virtually zero focus on individual treat/snack products or chews.
4. **K9 of Mine**
   - *Block Order*: Hero -> Buying Guides -> Training Tips -> Sidebar Ads.
   - *Gaps*: Ad-heavy, cluttered UI. Treats are often bundled into general training articles.
5. **PawDiet**
   - *Block Order*: Calculators -> Recalls -> Food Reviews.
   - *Gaps*: No specific snack calculator. Lacks deep dive reviews on single-ingredient treats.
6. **Great Pet Care**
   - *Block Order*: Hero -> Vet Reviewed -> Categories -> Trending.
   - *Gaps*: Medical focus is good, but lacks practical tradeoff comparisons for everyday owners.
7. **Bully Bunch (E-com Blog)**
   - *Block Order*: Shop -> Blog -> Social.
   - *Gaps*: Heavily biased towards their own products (bully sticks). Not objective.

## Positioning
**Chosen:** The most objective, data-driven, and veterinary-sourced guide to what you feed your dog between meals.
**Rejected 1:** E-commerce store selling our own treats. (Too biased)
**Rejected 2:** A general dog training blog. (Too broad)
**Rejected 3:** A crowd-sourced review site like Yelp for treats. (Unreliable)
**Rejected 4:** A holistic-only "raw food" zealot site. (Alienates average owners)

## Voice and Banned Words
**Voice:** Authoritative, empathetic, highly specific, plain English.
**Banned words:** "Furbaby", "Pup", "Doggos", "Superfood", "Miracle", "Guilt-free", "Click here", "Read on to find out".
