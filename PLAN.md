# Practice Strategy & Content Specification: Dr. Ameena Endocrinology

## 1. Patient Search Language & Query Intent
Patients rarely search using formal ICD-10 medical terminology. Instead, they search by daily symptoms, fears, lab result confusion, and local care needs:
- **Symptom Phrasing vs. Medical Terminology**:
  - *"Why am I so tired and gaining weight?"* -> Hypothyroidism / Subclinical Hypothyroidism
  - *"Shaky, nervous, heart racing after meal"* -> Hyperthyroidism / Reactive Hypoglycemia
  - *"A1c is 6.1 am I diabetic?"* -> Prediabetes / Impaired Fasting Glucose
  - *"Irregular periods, chin hair, weight won't budge"* -> Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)
  - *"Neck fullness when swallowing, lump in neck"* -> Thyroid Nodules / Goiter
  - *"Is thyroid biopsy painful?"* -> Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) Biopsy
- **Search Intent Buckets**:
  - **Informational**: Understanding symptoms, lab value ranges (e.g., TSH 4.5, A1C 5.9), medication side effects.
  - **Navigational**: Practice location, patient portal, downloadable preparation forms, direct office contact.
  - **Transactional / Local**: "Endocrinologist Austin TX accepting new patients", "PCOS specialist near me", "booking continuous glucose monitor consultation".

## 2. Competitor Doctor Sites Analysis
- **Common Competitor Shortcomings**:
  - **Overly Commercialized / Salesy**: Heavy callouts pushing unverified anti-aging supplements, aesthetic procedures, or cash-only concierge fees that undermine clinical trust.
  - **Generic Medical Blue Templates**: Cold corporate blues (`#0000FF`, `#0066CC`), stock photos of smiling actors in pristine lab coats posing with stethoscopes.
  - **Content Thinness**: Superficial 200-word summaries for complex conditions without diagnostic thresholds, citations, or last-reviewed dates.
  - **Poor Mobile Experience**: Cluttered navigation menus, non-responsive tables for lab values, non-accessible interactive forms.
- **Dr. Ameena's Strategic Differentiation**:
  - **Warm Botanical Palette**: Natural, calming palette (deep botanical green, sage, warm terracotta accent, soft cream background) that feels human, tranquil, and clinically grounded.
  - **Zero Stock Patients**: Typography-driven design, anatomical SVG vector illustrations, clear data tables, and authentic practice badges.
  - **Evidence-Led Depth**: Every condition and treatment page contains specific diagnostic thresholds (e.g., TSH > 4.5 mIU/L, A1C ≥ 6.5%), guidelines from major medical societies (Endocrine Society, ADA, AACE), peer-reviewed PubMed citations, and explicit review timestamps.
  - **HIPAA-Compliant Functional Tools**: Interactive symptom checklists and question builders that process data locally on the user's client device without storing or transmitting personal health information (PHI).

## 4. E-E-A-T & HIPAA Constraints
- **E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)**:
  - **Medical Review Badges**: Every clinical article carries an explicit badge: `Medically Reviewed by Dr. Ameena, MD, FACP [NEEDS CONFIRMATION] | Updated: [Date]`.
  - **Peer-Reviewed Citations**: Bulleted reference sections with DOI / PMIDs pointing to major clinical trials and society position papers.
  - **Transparent Credentials**: Clear distinction between board certification status, medical school, fellowship training, and hospital affiliations (all tagged `[NEEDS CONFIRMATION]`).
- **HIPAA & Patient Privacy Constraints**:
  - **Zero PHI Collection**: No interactive tool (Symptom Checklist, Question Builder, Insurance Checker) transmits, logs, or stores patient responses to any backend server or third-party tracker. All computations run in the browser's local memory.
  - **Form Privacy Warnings**: Notice on booking and inquiry pages reminding patients not to send urgent medical issues or confidential health details via web forms.
  - **Medical Disclaimer Notice**: Standard sticky/footer disclaimer across all content: *"The content on doctorameena.com is for educational purposes only and does not constitute formal medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Always consult with a qualified physician."*
