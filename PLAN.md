## Teardown of Competitors
1. **Adobe Acrobat Online**: Pushes hard to desktop app/subscription. High trust, high friction.
2. **Smallpdf**: Colorful, easy, but strict daily limits (2 files).
3. **Sejda**: Text-heavy homepage, decent free tier (3 tasks/hour, 200 pages), bit technical.
4. **ILovePDF**: Icon grid layout. Great for basic tasks, less focus on form-filling specifically.
5. **PDFescape**: Looks like Windows 95, but truly free and works well for basic editing.
6. **PDFfiller (airSlate)**: Aggressive SEO, looks like a generic corporate tool.
7. **DocHub**: Integrates well with G Suite, but UI can be overwhelming.

**Biggest Gap**: Most tools are either extremely limited on the free tier (nagging for upgrades immediately) or look completely outdated. None of them position themselves purely as "the tool that just works for fillable forms."

## Positioning
**Positioning**: The fast, bullshit-free tool to fill, sign, and export PDF forms. No watermarks on the free tier, no daily limits on basic filling. Premium is for advanced e-signatures and bulk exports.

**Rejected Alternatives**:
1. "The ultimate PDF suite" (Too broad, competing directly with Adobe).
2. "AI-powered document intelligence" (Buzzwordy, users just want to type their name in a box).
3. "The cheapest PDF editor" (Race to the bottom).
4. "Enterprise document workflows" (Wrong audience).
