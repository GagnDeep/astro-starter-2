  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "What is a Certified Food Protection Manager?"
    text_content: |
      A Certified Food Protection Manager (CFPM) is an individual who has passed an ANSI-accredited exam demonstrating advanced knowledge of food safety, microbiology, and hazard analysis critical control point (HACCP) principles. The FDA Food Code requires that at least one CFPM be present as the Person in Charge (PIC) during all hours of operation, including off-site catering events.

      This is not the basic food handler card obtained by taking a short online quiz. The CFPM exam requires proctoring, either in-person or via secure online monitoring, and covers complex topics like cooling parameters, biological hazards, and regulatory compliance.
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "Why do caterers need a Person in Charge at every event?"
    text_content: |
      Caterers need a Person in Charge at every event because health departments view off-site service as an extension of the primary commissary, requiring the same level of on-site managerial oversight to prevent foodborne illness outbreaks. If a health inspector visits your event and no CFPM is present, they can immediately shut down service and revoke your temporary permit.

      For large catering companies running multiple simultaneous events, this means you cannot rely on just the owner having the certification. Every event captain or lead chef must hold their own valid CFPM certificate.

    heading: "How do you prepare for the CFPM exam?"
    text_content: |
      You prepare for the CFPM exam by taking an accredited instruction course, studying temperature danger zones, cross-contamination prevention, and proper sanitizing chemical concentrations, and completing practice exams before the proctored test. While the instruction course is optional in some states, attempting the exam without formal study often results in failure due to the highly specific regulatory questions.

  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "What happens if a CFPM certification expires?"
    text_content: |
      If a CFPM certification expires, the individual is no longer legally recognized as a Person in Charge, and the catering business may face fines or shutdown if they operate without a certified manager on-site. Certifications typically last five years, and there is no grace period; you must pass the exam again before the expiration date.

      Health inspectors routinely check expiration dates during inspections. Operating with an expired certificate demonstrates negligence and can trigger a deeper, more punitive audit of your entire food safety protocol.
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "Can you take the food manager exam online?"
    text_content: |
      You can take the food manager exam online only if it is administered through a secure, proctored system that monitors your webcam, screen, and audio to prevent cheating, as required by ANSI accreditation standards. Unproctored online quizzes are only valid for basic food handler cards, not manager-level certification.

      The online proctored exam offers great flexibility for busy catering managers, but you must ensure your testing environment is completely private, quiet, and free of any reference materials.
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "Who pays for the food protection manager exam?"
    text_content: |
      The employer typically pays for the food protection manager exam, as it is a mandatory legal requirement for the business to operate legally off-site. Investing in your staff’s certification builds loyalty and ensures you have a deep bench of qualified event captains who can act as the Person in Charge during simultaneous weekend gigs.

      If an employee leaves shortly after obtaining the certification, the certificate generally belongs to the individual, not the business. Some caterers use training contracts requiring repayment if the employee quits within six months.
