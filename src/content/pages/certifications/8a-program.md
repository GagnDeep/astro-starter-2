    heading: What is the 8(a) Program?
    text_content: >
      <div id="guide"></div>
      The 8(a) Business Development program is a robust nine-year business assistance program created to help firms owned and controlled by socially and economically disadvantaged individuals. The federal government aims to award at least 5% of all federal contracting dollars to small disadvantaged businesses each year.


      Unlike other certifications that you hold indefinitely as long as you meet the criteria, the 8(a) program has a strict term limit. It is divided into a four-year developmental stage and a five-year transitional stage. Participants are subject to annual reviews and must maintain a balance between commercial and government revenue during the transitional stage.


      ### What defines a socially disadvantaged individual?

      Socially disadvantaged individuals are those who have been subjected to racial or ethnic prejudice or cultural bias within American society because of their identities as members of groups and without regard to their individual qualities.


      The SBA presumes that certain groups are socially disadvantaged, including Black Americans, Hispanic Americans, Native Americans, Asian Pacific Americans, and Subcontinent Asian Americans. Individuals who do not belong to one of these presumed groups can still qualify if they can establish individual social disadvantage by a preponderance of the evidence.


      ### What are the economic disadvantage criteria?

      To prove economic disadvantage, the individual claiming disadvantage must meet strict financial thresholds. Their personal net worth must be less than $850,000 (excluding the equity in their primary residence and the value of their ownership interest in the applicant business).


      Additionally, their adjusted gross income (AGI) averaged over the three preceding years cannot exceed $400,000, and the fair market value of all their assets (including their primary residence and the business) cannot exceed $6.5 million. The business itself must also demonstrate potential for success by having been in business in its primary industry for at least two full years.


      ### How does the 9-year program structure work?

      The nine-year program is structured to gradually reduce a firm's reliance on 8(a) contracts and prepare them to compete in the open market. During the initial four-year developmental stage, firms receive extensive business development assistance and are eligible for sole-source contracts.


      During the five-year transitional stage, firms must hit specific targets for non-8(a) revenue. If a firm fails to meet these competitive business mix targets, the SBA may restrict their ability to receive new sole-source 8(a) contracts. This ensures that firms do not become entirely dependent on the program and can survive after they graduate.
