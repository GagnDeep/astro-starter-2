  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "How do you calculate catering food cost percentage?"
    text_content: |
      Catering food cost percentage should target 25% to 28% of gross sales. To calculate it, divide the total cost of ingredients for an event by the total menu price charged to the client. Because catering involves pre-ordered menus with exact guest counts, you can control waste much more tightly than a retail restaurant, allowing for better margin realization.

      Never estimate. Build a master ingredient list and cost out every recipe down to the penny. Update these costs monthly to account for supply chain fluctuations.
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "What is the standard profit margin for a catering business?"
    text_content: |
      The standard net profit margin for a well-run catering business ranges from 10% to 15%. This is notably higher than the 3% to 5% typical of full-service restaurants. The improved margin stems from guaranteed headcounts, eliminated daily food waste, and scalable labor models where staff are hired strictly for contracted event hours.

      [Read more about catering profit margins](/pricing/margins/)
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "How should you price labor for an off-site event?"
    text_content: |
      Labor for off-site events should be billed directly to the client as a separate line item, rather than baked into the food price. Calculate the total hourly cost of your captains, servers, bartenders, and kitchen staff, plus a 20% to 30% markup to cover payroll taxes, workers' compensation, and administrative booking time.
