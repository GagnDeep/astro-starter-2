      <div id="guide"></div>
      Registering in the System for Award Management (SAM.gov) is entirely free. Despite the aggressive marketing of third-party services that charge hundreds or thousands of dollars to complete this registration for you, the process is designed to be completed directly by the business owner.


      This guide details the exact information you need to gather before you begin, the steps to create your account, and how to maintain your active status. You must renew your SAM registration annually to remain eligible for federal contracts.


      ### What documents are required for SAM registration?

      Before you start the registration process, you must gather your core business information. This includes your Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) or Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS, your banking information for Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT), and your Unique Entity ID (UEI).


      The UEI is a 12-character alphanumeric ID assigned to an entity by SAM.gov. As of April 2022, the federal government transitioned away from the DUNS number and now exclusively uses the UEI. You will request this ID directly within the SAM.gov portal during the first phase of your registration.


      ### How do you complete the core data sections?

      The core data section of SAM requires you to input your general business information, including your physical address, mailing address, and start date. You will also need to provide your Commercial and Government Entity (CAGE) Code. If you do not have a CAGE code, one will be assigned to you by the Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) during the registration process.


      You must accurately represent your business type and size. The government relies on your representations and certifications to determine your eligibility for small business set-asides. You will be asked to affirm your status under the specific NAICS codes you select for your business.


      ### What are the assertions and representations?

      In the assertions section, you will document the specific goods or services your business provides by selecting the appropriate NAICS codes and Product and Service Codes (PSC). You should identify one primary NAICS code that represents your core business, but you can select multiple secondary codes.


      The representations and certifications section (Reps & Certs) requires you to complete a lengthy questionnaire affirming your compliance with various federal regulations. This includes confirming your small business status, your compliance with labor laws, and your agreement to specific contractual clauses. You must read these questions carefully and answer them truthfully, as providing false information carries severe penalties.
