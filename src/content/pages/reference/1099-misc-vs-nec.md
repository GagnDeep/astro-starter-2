  - _name: TextBlock
    heading: The Short Answer
    text_content: >-
      Starting in the 2020 tax year, the IRS revived **Form 1099-NEC** (Nonemployee Compensation) specifically for freelance, contract, and gig work.


      If you performed a service as an independent contractor and were paid $600 or more during the year, you should receive a 1099-NEC.


      **Form 1099-MISC** is now primarily used for *other* types of income, such as rent, prizes, awards, or medical payments.

  - _name: TextBlock
    heading: Form Comparison Table
    text_content: >-
      | Feature | 1099-NEC | 1099-MISC |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | **What it reports** | Nonemployee compensation (freelance/contract work) | Rent, prizes, awards, medical payments |
      | **Reporting Threshold** | $600+ | $600+ (usually) |
      | **Common Recipients** | Freelancers, Uber drivers, Consultants | Landlords, Sweepstakes winners |
      | **Where you report it** | Schedule C (Subject to Self-Employment Tax) | Varies (Schedule E for rent, Schedule 1 for prizes) |

  - _name: TextBlock
    heading: Do I have to report income if I didn't get a 1099?
    text_content: >-
      **Yes.** This is the most common mistake freelancers make. The $600 threshold is the rule for the *business paying you*—it determines whether they are required to send the form.


      However, the rule for *you* (the taxpayer) is that you must report **all** income earned, even if it was just $50 and you never received a form. The IRS considers all freelance income taxable. Use our [1099 Tax Estimator](/tools/1099-estimator) to see what you might owe.

  - _name: TextBlock
    heading: How it affects your taxes
    text_content: >-
      Income reported in Box 1 of a 1099-NEC is considered self-employment income. This means you owe both standard Income Tax **AND** Self-Employment Tax (which covers Medicare and Social Security, currently 15.3% for 2024).


      Because you are self-employed, you can deduct ordinary and necessary business expenses to lower your taxable profit. You will report the income and deduct your expenses on **Schedule C** of your Form 1040.

  - _name: TextBlock
    heading: FAQs
    text_content: >-
      **Q: I paid a freelancer via credit card or PayPal. Do I send them a 1099-NEC?**

      A: No. Payments made with a credit card or payment settlement entity (like PayPal or Stripe) are reported by those companies on a **1099-K**. If you send them a 1099-NEC as well, their income will be reported to the IRS twice.


      **Q: What if a client sent me a 1099-MISC instead of a 1099-NEC for freelance work?**

      A: Many small business owners are still confused by the change. If the income is definitely freelance work, you can still report it on Schedule C. However, it's best practice to ask the client to issue a corrected form.
