  - _name: FaqAccordion
    heading: Frequently asked questions about mocking LLMs
    items:
      - question: How do you mock non-deterministic LLM responses?
        answer: To mock non-deterministic LLM responses, you must record a known-good interaction once, and replay it exactly. JsonMock acts as a proxy during your initial test run, saving the request and the real API's response to a fixture file. In subsequent runs, the proxy intercepts the matching request and serves the saved response, guaranteeing determinism.
      - question: Can I just write JSON fixtures by hand?
        answer: Writing JSON fixtures by hand for LLMs is brittle and time-consuming. Modern API payloads often exceed hundreds of kilobytes and include complex tool-call structures or nested base64 strings. When the provider updates their schema, your hand-written fixtures silently drift, causing tests to pass locally but fail in production. Record-and-replay ensures accuracy.
      - question: How does this work with streaming (SSE)?
        answer: Mocking Server-Sent Events requires a specialized server that can simulate the precise timing and chunking of a real stream. JsonMock records the exact byte boundaries and delays of the original stream, replaying them accurately so your frontend UI logic can be tested for partial-rendering bugs.
  - _name: TextBlock
    heading: Why not just use VCR or Wiremock?
    text_content: General-purpose HTTP mocking tools were not designed for the payloads generated by modern LLMs. VCR and PollyJS tie you to specific programming languages, breaking down when your orchestration layer is Python but your frontend is TypeScript. JsonMock sits at the network level as a language-agnostic proxy, specifically optimized for the structure and latency profile of AI APIs.
