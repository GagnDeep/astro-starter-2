      A gap analysis is the foundation of your battery passport strategy. It highlights the distance between your current capabilities and the mandatory requirements of the EU Battery Regulation.

      ### Step 1: Data Mapping against EU Requirements

      Begin by mapping your existing data against the specific fields required by the regulation. This includes:
      - Carbon footprint data (primary vs. secondary)
      - Material composition and recycled content percentages
      - Supply chain due diligence documentation

      Identify what data you have, what data you need to source from suppliers, and what data currently does not exist.

      ### Step 2: Process and Governance Review

      Evaluate your internal processes. Do you have cross-functional alignment? The battery passport requires input from procurement, engineering, sustainability, and IT. A siloed approach will fail.

      ### Step 3: Technical Infrastructure Assessment

      Assess your IT systems. Can your current architecture handle the secure aggregation and sharing of granular product data? You will need systems capable of generating decentralized identifiers (DIDs) and managing verifiable credentials to meet the EU's technical specifications.
