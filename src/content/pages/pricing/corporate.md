  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "How should you price corporate drop-off catering?"
    text_content: |
      Corporate drop-off catering should be priced on a strict per-head basis, incorporating food, packaging, and basic administrative overhead into the menu price. Delivery fees should always be itemized separately, calculated by distance and setup time, rather than baked into the food cost. This protects your margins when serving smaller orders at distant office parks.

      Implement strict minimum order amounts. A $500 minimum ensures that the labor required to prep, pack, and dispatch a driver does not eclipse the profit margin of the order itself.
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "How do you handle corporate volume discounts?"
    text_content: |
      Corporate volume discounts must be structured on a recurring commitment basis, not a per-order basis. Do not offer a 10% discount just because a client orders for 200 people once. Instead, offer a 5% to 10% discount to clients who sign a contract for weekly recurring orders. This guarantees predictable revenue and justifies the margin reduction.
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "What hidden costs erode corporate drop-off margins?"
    text_content: |
      Hidden costs that erode corporate drop-off margins include high-end disposable packaging, chafing dish fuel (Sternos), and driver wait times at security desks. Always cost out the exact price of the clamshells, cutlery kits, and bags used per order, and factor them into your baseline overhead calculations.
