      ## What is the core regulatory purpose of the Series 65 licensing examination?

      Before you can provide investment advice for a fee, you must be properly licensed as an Investment Adviser Representative (IAR). The primary mechanism for demonstrating competency to state regulators is the Uniform Investment Adviser Law Examination, commonly known as the Series 65.

      For breakaway advisers coming from a broker-dealer environment, understanding how your existing licenses (like the Series 7 and Series 66) or your professional designations translate to an independent RIA is the first gating item in your transition timeline.

      ### Which specific roles within an RIA require an active Series 65 registration?

      In general, anyone who will be providing investment advice, managing client portfolios, or soliciting clients on behalf of the RIA must hold a valid Series 65 license, unless they qualify for an exemption or waiver.

      The Series 65 is a 130-question exam covering economic concepts, investment vehicle characteristics, client investment recommendations, and—most importantly—laws, regulations, and guidelines, including the Uniform Securities Act and the Investment Advisers Act of 1940.

      ### Which active professional designations automatically grant a waiver from the Series 65 requirement?

      Most state securities administrators grant waivers from the Series 65 examination requirement for individuals who currently hold and maintain one of the following professional designations in good standing:

      - **CFP:** Certified Financial Planner
      - **CFA:** Chartered Financial Analyst
      - **CIC:** Chartered Investment Counselor
      - **ChFC:** Chartered Financial Consultant
      - **PFS:** Personal Financial Specialist

      If you hold one of these designations, you will generally only need to file a Form U4 and pay the associated registration fees to become licensed. However, it is imperative to verify this waiver directly with the securities division of your specific state, as some states have nuanced rules regarding the active status of these designations.

      ### Does holding a valid Series 7 and Series 66 combination satisfy the requirements for an independent IAR?

      If you are currently registered at a broker-dealer and hold both a valid Series 7 (General Securities Representative) and a valid Series 66 (Uniform Combined State Law Examination), you typically satisfy the licensing requirement for an IAR. The Series 66 functions as a combination of the Series 63 and Series 65.

      Crucially, if you leave your broker-dealer, your Series 7 will eventually expire (usually after two years). If your Series 7 lapses, your Series 66 may no longer be sufficient on its own to maintain your IAR registration, depending on the state. In such cases, you may be forced to take the Series 65 if you lack one of the qualifying professional designations.
