Your choice often comes down to your service model and how much you value restaurant-specific hardware versus ecosystem flexibility.

    heading: "Which system has better hardware for restaurants?"
    text_content: |
      Toast's hardware is famously ruggedized. It is designed to withstand the heat, grease, and spills typical of a commercial kitchen. Because they control both the hardware and the Android OS, updates are generally more stable and less prone to breaking mid-service.

      Square primarily relies on consumer iPads or their proprietary Square Register. While iPads are sleek and cost-effective, they are not inherently waterproof or drop-proof. However, if a Square iPad dies on a Friday night, you can run to an electronics store and replace it instantly. Toast hardware requires overnight shipping from the vendor.

  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "How do processing fees compare?"
    text_content: |
      Processing fees are the hidden anchor of POS systems. Both platforms strongly encourage (or require) you to use their in-house payment processing.

      Square traditionally offers transparent flat-rate pricing (e.g., 2.6% + 10¢ per dip/tap). This is excellent for low-volume cafes but can be punitive for high-volume restaurants with large average checks.

      Toast often quotes custom rates based on your projected volume. While they offer flat rates, high-volume operators can sometimes negotiate better interchange-plus rates. However, you must carefully read your contract to ensure rates are locked in and not subject to arbitrary hikes. Always negotiate based on your specific average ticket size and monthly volume.
