# Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions below. Can't find what you're looking for? [Contact us](/contact).

## Investing Basics

### Do I need a 20% deposit?
Not necessarily. While a 20% deposit avoids [Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI)](/glossary/lmi), many investors buy with 10% or even 5% deposits to enter the market sooner. The trade-off is paying the insurance premium.

### What is a good rental yield?
It depends on the location and property type. As a general rule, a gross yield of 4-5% in a major capital city is considered strong. Regional areas often offer higher yields (6%+) but may have lower long-term capital growth. Calculate yours using our [Rental Yield Calculator](/tools/rental-yield-calculator).

### Should I buy a house or an apartment?
Historically, houses (land) appreciate faster than apartments because "land appreciates, buildings depreciate". However, apartments can offer better cashflow and lower entry prices. Read more in our guide on [Residential vs Commercial](/library/residential-vs-commercial) (which also touches on property types).

## Finance

### How does equity work?
Equity is the difference between your property's value and your mortgage balance. If you have enough, you can borrow against it to fund the deposit for another property. See our [Useable Equity Calculator](/tools/equity-calculator).

### Should I choose interest-only or principal and interest?
Investors often choose [Interest Only](/glossary/interest-only) loans to minimize their out-of-pocket holding costs and maximize tax deductions (as only interest is tax-deductible). However, P&I loans generally offer lower interest rates.

## Strategy

### What is rentvesting?
[Rentvesting](/glossary/rentvesting) is a strategy where you rent a property to live in (usually in an area you want to live but can't afford to buy) and buy an investment property in an area you can afford.
