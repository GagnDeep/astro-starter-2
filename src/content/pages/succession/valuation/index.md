      ## How is the enterprise value of an independent RIA mathematically determined?

      Valuing a Registered Investment Adviser (RIA) is an exercise in projecting sustainable, recurring cash flow. Unlike traditional businesses where physical assets drive value, an RIA's value is derived almost entirely from the stickiness of its client relationships and the predictability of its fee revenue.

      We focus exclusively on the mathematics of valuation and client retention, stripping away the optimistic multiples often cited by M&A aggregators. A true valuation must account for transition risk, client age demographics, and the structural profitability of the firm.

      ### Why have sophisticated buyers shifted from revenue multiples to adjusted EBITDA?

      Historically, smaller practices (under $200M AUM) were valued on a multiple of trailing twelve months (TTM) recurring revenue—often ranging from 2.0x to 3.0x. This rule of thumb is increasingly obsolete.

      Sophisticated buyers, particularly private equity-backed aggregators, value firms based on Adjusted EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization). This shift forces sellers to demonstrate actual profitability, not just top-line growth.

      - **Revenue Multiples:** Still used for smaller, lifestyle practices where the buyer intends to absorb the clients and eliminate the seller's overhead entirely.
      - **EBITDA Multiples:** The standard for enterprise firms (typically >$500M AUM) with institutionalized processes, next-generation leadership, and scalable infrastructure. Multiples here can range from 6x to 15x+, depending heavily on organic growth rates.

      ### How does the average age of your client base directly discount your firm's valuation multiplier?

      A buyer is purchasing future cash flows. If your client base is predominantly over the age of 75 and in the distribution phase of their portfolios, those cash flows will inevitably decline as assets are drawn down or transferred to heirs (who often switch advisers).

      Firms with younger, accumulating clients command a premium. You must segment your revenue by client age and asset phase to determine a defensible growth rate for the valuation model.

      ### What structural mechanisms in an earn-out provision protect the buyer against post-transition client attrition?

      Rarely is an RIA sold for 100% cash at closing. The transaction structure dictates the true realized value.

      Most deals involve an upfront payment (e.g., 60% of enterprise value) with the remainder paid via an earn-out over 24 to 36 months. This earn-out is typically contingent on client retention and revenue targets. If you negotiate a high valuation but accept a deal structure heavily weighted toward aggressive growth hurdles, your realized value may fall far short of the headline number.

      The mathematics of post-transition client retention are brutal. A 10% client attrition rate during a transition can wipe out the entire upside of an earn-out provision. Your succession plan must prioritize client continuity above all else to protect your enterprise value.
