  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "What must a catering contract include?"
    text_content: |
      A catering contract must include detailed payment schedules, non-refundable deposit clauses, force majeure provisions, and explicit final headcount deadlines. A simple menu agreement is not enough; you need a legal document that protects your business from sudden cancellations, venue liability, and client scope creep.

      Every contract should specify the exact time the event ends and outline overtime hourly rates if the client requests extended service on the day of the event.
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "How do you write a catering cancellation policy?"
    text_content: |
      A catering cancellation policy should utilize a sliding scale for refunds based on the event's proximity. For example, cancellations 90 days out may forfeit only the initial deposit, while cancellations within 14 days forfeit 100% of the estimated total. This covers the sunk costs of food ordering and labor scheduling that cannot be reversed.

      [Learn more about cancellation policies](/contracts/cancellations/)
  - _name: "TextBlock"
    heading: "How do venue commission agreements work?"
    text_content: |
      Venue commission agreements require the caterer to pay the venue a percentage of gross food and beverage sales (typically 10% to 15%) for the right to operate on their property. Operators must decide whether to absorb this cost, impacting net margin, or explicitly pass it through as a "Venue Administrative Fee" on the client's invoice.
