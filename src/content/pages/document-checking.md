      ### Why Self-Checking is Insufficient
      It is entirely natural to assume that if you have read the official guidelines and filled out the forms, your application is ready to go. You might carefully check your work, cross-referencing your documents against the VFS checklist. However, the flaw in this approach is that you are checking your work against the *published* rules, not the *enforced* rules.

      The reality at VFS application centres is that agents enforce a strict, often unwritten interpretation of the guidelines. They operate on zero tolerance for ambiguity. A document that seems perfectly acceptable to you might be rejected because of a missing stamp, an incorrect date format, or a slight variation in name spelling. Self-checking suffers from confirmation bias; you see what you expect to see. Our professional audit provides the necessary objective scrutiny. We look at your application not as you intended it, but exactly as a VFS agent will see it.

      ### The Anatomy of a Document Audit
      Our document checking service is not a cursory glance; it is a rigorous, line-by-line audit of your entire application package. The process begins with the foundational documents: your application forms. We verify that every field is completed, that the information is consistent across all pages, and that the formatting matches official expectations. We check for common errors, such as confusing the 'Given Name' and 'Surname' fields—a surprisingly frequent mistake that mandates a complete re-submission.

      Next, we scrutinize your supporting documents. This is where the majority of rejections occur. We do not just check that a document is present; we verify its provenance, its validity, and its formatting. If a document requires notarisation, we ensure the notary's seal and signature meet the accepted standards. If a document requires an apostille, we verify that the apostille is attached correctly and issued by a competent authority. This granular level of detail is what guarantees your application's success.

      ### The Photographic Minefield
      Photographs are arguably the most common cause of application rejection. The Indian government has extremely specific, inflexible requirements for passport and OCI photographs. They dictate the exact dimensions (usually 51mm x 51mm), the background color (plain white or off-white), the size of the head within the frame, and even the lighting.

      A photograph taken in a standard UK photo booth will almost always be rejected, as these booths are calibrated for UK passport standards, which differ significantly from Indian standards. A photograph printed on the wrong type of paper, or one with a slight shadow behind the ear, will be summarily dismissed. Our audit includes a precise digital or physical measurement of your photographs against these strict criteria. If your photos do not comply, we will tell you exactly why and what you need to instruct your photographer to do to fix them.
