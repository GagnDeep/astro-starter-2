## Understanding BMR

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the foundation of your metabolism. Tracking your food is the most reliable way to hit your goals, but to know *what* to track, you first must understand how much energy your body uses simply to stay alive.

### The Basics: What is BMR?

BMR represents the minimum number of calories your body requires to function at complete rest. Imagine you lay in bed all day without moving a muscle—your body still needs energy to pump blood, breathe, grow hair, blink, and maintain body temperature. That energy expenditure is your BMR.

For most people, BMR accounts for roughly **60% to 75%** of their Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).

### How BMR is Calculated

There are several formulas, but the **Mifflin-St Jeor equation** is widely regarded by dietitians as the most accurate standard formula. It requires your weight, height, age, and biological sex.

- **Men:** `10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age(y) + 5`
- **Women:** `10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) - 5 × age(y) - 161`

### Worked Example

Let's calculate the BMR for a 30-year-old female who weighs 65 kg (143 lbs) and is 165 cm (5'5") tall.

1. **Weight:** 10 × 65 = 650
2. **Height:** 6.25 × 165 = 1031.25
3. **Age:** 5 × 30 = 150
4. **Base Calculation:** 650 + 1031.25 - 150 = 1531.25
5. **Gender adjustment (Women):** 1531.25 - 161 = **1370.25 kcal**

Her BMR is approximately 1,370 calories per day.

### Factors That Influence BMR

- **Muscle Mass:** Muscle tissue is metabolically active. The more muscle you have, the higher your BMR. This is why resistance training is excellent for fat loss.
- **Age:** BMR naturally decreases as you age, largely due to a loss of muscle mass over time.
- **Body Size:** Larger bodies require more energy to maintain.

### Common Mistakes

1. **Eating below BMR:** Many people try to diet by eating 1,200 calories or less. If your BMR is 1,400 and your TDEE is 2,000, eating 1,200 calories is aggressive and often unsustainable. It is generally recommended not to eat below your BMR without medical supervision.
2. **Confusing BMR with TDEE:** Your BMR is just your resting rate. To figure out how much you should eat, you must multiply your BMR by an activity factor to get your TDEE.
