# Water Balance in the Human Body

Water makes up approximately 60% of the adult human body, but its distribution and regulation are highly complex. This guide explores the intricate details of fluid homeostasis.

## The Physiology of Fluid Balance

The human body relies on a delicate balance. The kidneys process roughly 180 liters of blood daily, reabsorbing most of the water and excreting about 1 to 2 liters as urine.

### Cellular Distribution
Water is distributed across two main compartments: the [Intracellular Fluid](/glossary/intracellular-fluid) (inside cells) and the [Extracellular Fluid](/glossary/extracellular-fluid) (outside cells).

## Environmental Factors
Several elements influence this process:
1. **Environmental Temperature:** High heat increases sweat loss.
2. **Activity Level:** Muscular exertion requires greater fluid turnover.
3. **Dietary Intake:** Sodium and potassium levels dictate fluid retention.

## Evidence and Guidelines
According to the U.S. National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2004), baseline adequate intake is:

| Demographic | Adequate Intake (Liters/day) |
|-------------|------------------------------|
| Men (Adults) | 3.7 |
| Women (Adults) | 2.7 |

## Common Mistakes
A frequent error is assuming that [Thirst Mechanism](/glossary/thirst-mechanism) is a proactive signal. In reality, by the time thirst is triggered, the body is already mildly dehydrated.
