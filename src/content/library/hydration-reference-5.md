# The Ultimate Deep Dive on Hydration Science - Part 5

Water is the foundation of life, making up roughly 60% of the adult human body. It acts as a building block for cells, regulates our internal body temperature, transports carbohydrates and proteins in the bloodstream, and helps flush waste mainly through urination.

## The Physiology of Hydration

When you drink water, it is absorbed primarily in the small intestine, though some absorption begins in the stomach. The rate of absorption depends heavily on the volume of fluid ingested and its composition.

### Fluid Balance and Osmolality

The body meticulously controls fluid balance through a complex system involving the kidneys, the hypothalamus in the brain, and various hormones like antidiuretic hormone (ADH). When you sweat or lose fluid, the concentration of particles in your blood (osmolality) increases. This triggers the sensation of thirst and signals the kidneys to conserve water.

## Evidence-Based Guidelines

According to the U.S. National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2004), the adequate daily fluid intake is:

| Demographic | Adequate Intake (Liters/day) |
|-------------|------------------------------|
| Men (Adults) | 3.7 |
| Women (Adults) | 2.7 |
| Pregnant Women | 3.0 |

*Note: This includes fluids from water, other beverages, and food (which typically provides about 20% of daily fluid intake).*

### Common Mistakes and Edge Cases

1. **Overhydration (Hyponatremia):** Drinking too much water in a short period without replacing electrolytes can dangerously dilute blood sodium levels. This is common in endurance athletes who drink excessive plain water.
2. **Ignoring Food as a Water Source:** Fruits like watermelon and cucumbers are over 90% water.
3. **Wait Until Thirsty:** By the time you feel thirsty, you may already be mildly dehydrated.
