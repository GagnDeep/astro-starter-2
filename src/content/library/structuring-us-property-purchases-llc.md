## Overview

When purchasing US real estate, the name on the title dictates your liability exposure, tax requirements, and estate planning ease. For UAE residents and Gulf-based investors, purchasing US property in your personal name is almost always a costly mistake.

The preferred method is holding the asset within a Limited Liability Company (LLC), often layered with foreign corporate structures depending on the size of the portfolio.

### The Risks of Personal Ownership

1. **Unlimited Liability:** The US is a highly litigious society. If a tenant or visitor is injured on your property and sues, holding the property in your personal name means all of your global assets could theoretically be targeted in a lawsuit.
2. **Estate Tax Exposure:** As discussed in our [Tax Implications Guide](/library/tax-implications-us-real-estate-uae-residents/), Non-Resident Aliens holding US real estate in their personal name face a 40% estate tax on the asset's value above $60,000 upon their passing.
3. **Loss of Privacy:** Property records in the US are public. Anyone can look up the owner of a property, their mailing address, and the purchase price.

### Why Use a US LLC?

A US LLC acts as a legal barrier between the property and the owner.

- **Asset Protection:** The LLC limits your liability to the assets held within that specific LLC. If the LLC is sued, your personal assets (and properties in other LLCs) are protected.
- **Privacy:** Certain states allow for anonymous LLCs, keeping your name off public records.
- **Ease of Transfer:** It is often easier to transfer ownership of an LLC to an heir or partner than it is to transfer the deed to the property itself.

### Choosing the Right State: Wyoming vs. Delaware

You do not have to form the LLC in the state where the property is located. For holding companies, UAE investors typically choose between Wyoming and Delaware.

| Feature | Wyoming LLC | Delaware LLC |
|---|---|---|
| **Privacy** | High (Members not on public record) | High (Members not on public record) |
| **Annual Fees** | Low (~$60/year) | Medium ($300/year Franchise Tax) |
| **Legal Precedent** | Strong charging order protection | The gold standard for corporate law |
| **Best For** | Single properties, small portfolios | Large syndications, institutional capital |

*Note: If you buy a property in Florida using a Wyoming LLC, you must still register the Wyoming LLC as a "Foreign Entity" doing business in Florida.*

### The "Blocker" Structure for High Net Worth Investors

While a standard US LLC provides liability protection, a single-member LLC is "disregarded" by the IRS for tax purposes. This means it **does not protect against the US Estate Tax.**

To avoid the 40% estate tax, high-net-worth investors often use a "Blocker" structure:
1. The Investor (in the UAE) owns 100% of a Foreign Corporation (e.g., a BVI or Cayman company, or a UAE Freezone company depending on specific treaty interpretations).
2. The Foreign Corporation owns 100% of a US LLC (e.g., a Wyoming LLC).
3. The US LLC holds the title to the real estate.

Upon the investor's death, they transfer shares in the *Foreign Corporation*. Because the US asset is owned by a corporation that never dies, the US Estate Tax is not triggered.

### Step-by-Step Entity Formation

1. **Select the State:** Choose Wyoming or Delaware for the holding company.
2. **Engage a Registered Agent:** An LLC must have a physical address in the state of formation to receive legal documents. We provide this service.
3. **File Articles of Organization:** Submit the required paperwork to the state.
4. **Obtain an EIN:** Apply for an Employer Identification Number from the IRS. (This can take weeks for foreign nationals without an SSN or ITIN, so start early).
5. **Open a US Bank Account:** The LLC must have its own bank account to maintain the "corporate veil." Comingling personal and rental funds destroys the liability protection.

### Next Steps

Structuring should be completed *before* you make an offer on a property. Changing the title of a property after purchase can trigger "Due on Sale" clauses from lenders or incur transfer taxes.
