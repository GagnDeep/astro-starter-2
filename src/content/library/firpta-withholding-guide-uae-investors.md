## Overview

When a foreign person sells real estate in the United States, they are subject to the **Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act (FIRPTA)**.

FIRPTA is not a tax itself; rather, it is a *withholding mechanism* designed to ensure that foreign sellers pay their capital gains tax before moving the proceeds out of the US (e.g., back to Dubai or Abu Dhabi).

For UAE residents, understanding FIRPTA is crucial because it directly impacts the liquidity you receive at closing.

### How FIRPTA Works

Under FIRPTA, the **buyer** of the property is legally responsible for withholding a percentage of the **Gross Sales Price** and remitting it to the IRS within 20 days of the closing.

*It is vital to understand that the withholding is based on the total sale price, not your profit (capital gain).*

**The Standard Withholding Rate is 15%.**

### Worked Example

Imagine a UAE resident who bought a condo in Miami for $400,000 and sells it three years later for $500,000.
- **Gross Sale Price:** $500,000
- **Actual Profit (Gain):** $100,000
- **FIRPTA Withholding (15% of $500k):** **$75,000**

At closing, the title company will hold back $75,000 and send it to the IRS. The seller only receives $425,000 (minus closing costs and mortgage payoffs).

Even though the actual capital gains tax on the $100,000 profit might only be $15,000, the IRS holds the full $75,000 until a tax return is filed.

### How to Get Your Money Back (The Refund Process)

Because FIRPTA usually results in an overpayment of taxes, the foreign seller must file a US tax return (Form 1040-NR) in the following calendar year to report the actual capital gain, calculate the true tax owed, and claim a refund for the difference.

In the example above, the seller would eventually receive a refund of $60,000 ($75,000 withheld - $15,000 actual tax).

**The Catch:** This process can take 12 to 18 months, tying up a significant amount of your capital.

### Applying for a Withholding Certificate (Form 8288-B)

If you know the FIRPTA withholding will vastly exceed your actual tax liability (or if you are selling at a loss), you can apply for a **Withholding Certificate** from the IRS prior to closing.

If the IRS approves the certificate, the title company can hold the funds in escrow instead of sending them to the IRS, and release the excess funds to you much faster once the certificate is processed (usually 90-120 days).

*Requirement:* Both the buyer and seller must have US Taxpayer Identification Numbers (ITIN or SSN) to apply for this certificate.

### Exemptions to FIRPTA

1. **The $300,000 Exemption:** If the sale price is $300,000 or less, AND the buyer intends to use the property as a primary residence for at least 50% of the time over the next two years, the withholding rate is **0%**.
2. **The 10% Reduced Rate:** If the sale price is between $300,001 and $1,000,000, AND the buyer intends to use it as a primary residence, the withholding rate is reduced to **10%**.
3. **Corporate Ownership:** If the property is owned by a domestic US Corporation (a C-Corp), FIRPTA does not apply. *Note: A standard single-member [LLC](/library/structuring-us-property-purchases-llc/) is a disregarded entity, meaning FIRPTA STILL applies to the foreign owner of the LLC.*

### Strategic Advice for Gulf Investors

Do not wait until you have a buyer to think about FIRPTA.
- Ensure you have an ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) well before you intend to sell.
- Work with a CPA and title company experienced in cross-border transactions. A standard domestic title agent will often make mistakes with FIRPTA paperwork, leading to severe delays.
- Consider 1031 exchange equivalents if you plan to reinvest the capital into another US property.
