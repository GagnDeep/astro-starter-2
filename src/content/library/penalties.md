# EU Battery Regulation Penalties

The EU Battery Regulation (2023/1542) represents a shift from voluntary sustainability reporting to hard legal mandates. While the Regulation sets the rules at the European level, the enforcement and specific penalty structures are left to the individual Member States.

## The Ultimate Penalty: Market Exclusion

The most severe penalty for failing to comply with the core mandates—such as the Carbon Footprint Declaration or the Digital Product Passport—is not a fine, but **market exclusion**.

If a battery does not have a valid, accessible Digital Product Passport by the February 18, 2027 deadline (for EV and LMT batteries), it **cannot legally be placed on the market or put into service** anywhere within the European Union.

### The Mechanism of Exclusion

1.  **Customs Seizure:** For imported batteries, national customs authorities are empowered to halt shipments that lack the required documentation or conformity markings (including the QR code linked to the passport).
2.  **Product Recalls:** Market surveillance authorities in any Member State can order the recall of non-compliant batteries already in the supply chain.
3.  **B2B Rejection:** Major OEMs (automakers) will outright reject shipments of cells or packs from tier-1 suppliers that lack passport compliance, as the OEM assumes the legal risk when placing the final vehicle on the market.

## Financial Penalties

Article 88 of the Regulation requires Member States to lay down rules on penalties applicable to infringements. The text stipulates that penalties must be **"effective, proportionate and dissuasive."**

While exact euro amounts vary by country (often administered through national environmental or consumer protection agencies), they typically follow the structure of existing CE marking and REACH violations:

*   **Administrative Fines:** Ranging from tens of thousands to millions of euros, depending on the severity of the infringement and the size of the economic operator.
*   **Daily Non-Compliance Fines:** Fines levied for every day the non-compliance continues after a formal warning.

## Specific Infringement Scenarios

### 1. Missing or Incomplete Data
If a regulator scans the QR code and the required public data (e.g., carbon footprint) is missing, or if a recycler is improperly denied access to dismantling manuals, the operator will be issued a notice to rectify the issue within a specific timeframe before market restriction procedures begin.

### 2. Fraudulent Data (Greenwashing)
Submitting falsified carbon footprint data or forged certificates of origin for recycled content is considered a severe infringement. Because this data must be verified by a Notified Body, falsification may involve fraud charges under national law, carrying potential criminal liability for company directors, alongside massive fines.

### 3. Supply Chain Due Diligence Failures
Starting in August 2025, economic operators must have a verified supply chain due diligence policy regarding human rights and environmental risks (e.g., child labor in cobalt mining). Failure to implement this policy, or failing to act when risks are identified, will result in targeted penalties and potential exclusion from public procurement contracts.

## How Enforcement Works

1.  **Notified Bodies:** Independent third-party auditors (like TÜV, SGS, Dekra) are the first line of defense. They must verify your carbon footprint and recycled content declarations *before* you can issue a CE mark. If you fail their audit, you cannot sell the battery.
2.  **Market Surveillance Authorities (MSAs):** National agencies will conduct random spot-checks. They will physically scan QR codes on batteries in warehouses and dealerships, request access to the underlying databases, and cross-reference the data with customs declarations.
3.  **The EU Passport Registry:** The EU is building a central registry that will store the unique identifiers (DIDs) of all valid battery passports. MSAs will use this registry to rapidly identify fake or missing passports.

## What to do next

1.  **Identify your Notified Body:** Do not wait until 2026. Engage a Notified Body now to understand their specific audit requirements for the 2025 carbon footprint declaration.
2.  **Implement Data Governance:** Treat battery passport data with the same rigor as financial accounting. Implement strict internal sign-offs before data is published to the passport.
3.  **Review Contracts:** Ensure your supplier contracts have indemnification clauses holding them financially responsible if their failure to provide required data causes your product to be excluded from the EU market.
