# Battery Recycled Content Requirements

To drive a circular economy and reduce reliance on critical raw material imports, the EU Battery Regulation introduces mandatory recycled content targets. These apply to EV batteries, LMT batteries, and industrial batteries with a capacity greater than 2 kWh.

## The Mandate

Economic operators must declare the percentage of recycled cobalt, lead, lithium, and nickel present in the active materials of their batteries. This is a two-phased approach:

1. **Declaration Phase:** Starting August 18, 2028, batteries must carry a declaration of the recycled content share.
2. **Mandatory Targets Phase:** Starting August 18, 2033, batteries must meet minimum recycled content thresholds.

## Minimum Recycled Content Targets

| Material | Target (from Aug 18, 2033) | Target (from Aug 18, 2036) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cobalt** | 16% | 26% |
| **Lead** | 85% | 85% |
| **Lithium** | 6% | 12% |
| **Nickel** | 6% | 15% |

*Note: These targets apply to the active materials within the battery, not the entire weight of the battery pack (which includes aluminum casings, plastics, etc.).*

## Traceability and Mass Balance

Proving recycled content is complex because recycled materials are often blended with virgin materials at the smelter or precursor level.

The EU is expected to allow a **Chain of Custody (CoC)** model based on **Mass Balance**. This means that physical separation of recycled and virgin materials is not strictly required throughout the entire supply chain, provided that a robust accounting system tracks the inputs and outputs.

### How we track recycled content

To verify recycled content claims, economic operators will need:

1. **Certificates of Origin:** From recyclers proving the volume of secondary materials recovered from waste batteries.
2. **Allocation Rules:** Transparent accounting showing how the secondary material credits are allocated to specific batches of active materials (cathode/anode).
3. **Third-Party Verification:** A notified body must audit the mass balance accounting system to prevent double-counting.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing recovery rates with recycled content:** Recovery targets apply to recyclers (e.g., they must recover 90% of the cobalt from an old battery). Recycled content targets apply to new battery manufacturers (e.g., 16% of the cobalt in a *new* battery must be recycled).
- **Ignoring manufacturing scrap:** Pre-consumer waste (manufacturing scrap) *can* count towards recycled content targets under specific conditions, but it must be meticulously tracked to distinguish it from post-consumer waste.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**We need to track recycled content - cobalt, lithium, nickel - how?**
You will need a digital traceability platform that supports mass balance accounting across your tier-2 and tier-3 suppliers (smelters and refiners). Spreadsheets will not survive a notified body audit.

**Do we need an independent third-party verification for our supply chain data?**
Yes. The regulation mandates third-party conformity assessments to verify both carbon footprint and recycled content declarations.

## What to do next

1. **Assess your supply chain:** Ask your active material suppliers (cathode makers) about their plans to source secondary materials and their ability to provide mass balance certification.
2. **Review your contracts:** Ensure future supply agreements include clauses mandating the provision of verified recycled content data.
