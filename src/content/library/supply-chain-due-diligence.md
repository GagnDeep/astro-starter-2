# Supply Chain Due Diligence

The EU Battery Regulation extends the compliance boundary far beyond the factory walls. Under Article 48, economic operators placing batteries on the market must implement and maintain a comprehensive supply chain due diligence policy.

This is not a voluntary ESG pledge. It is a strict legal requirement, taking effect **August 18, 2025**.

## The Scope of Due Diligence

The regulation specifically targets the extraction, processing, and trading of critical raw materials: **cobalt, natural graphite, lithium, and nickel**, as well as chemical compounds containing them.

Your due diligence policy must assess two distinct categories of risk:

### 1. Social and Human Rights Risks
*   Child labor and forced labor (particularly relevant in ASM - Artisanal and Small-scale Mining).
*   Occupational health and safety.
*   Rights of indigenous peoples.
*   Community life, including conflicts over land and resources.

### 2. Environmental Risks
*   Air, water, and soil pollution.
*   Water usage and depletion.
*   Biodiversity loss.
*   Hazardous substance management.

## The 5-Step Due Diligence Framework

The EU mandate closely mirrors the OECD Due Diligence Guidance. Economic operators must demonstrate they are actively executing the following five steps:

### Step 1: Establish a Management System
You must adopt a formal company policy clearly stating your standards for the supply chain. Crucially, you must incorporate these standards into your **supplier contracts**. If a tier-2 supplier violates these standards, your contract must allow you to terminate the relationship.

### Step 2: Identify and Assess Risks
This is where the Battery Passport infrastructure becomes essential. You cannot assess risks if you do not know who your suppliers are. You must trace the origin of the critical materials back to the mine or recycling facility.

*The "We buy from a tier-1 distributor and don't know the mine" defense is legally invalid under the new regulation.*

### Step 3: Design and Implement a Strategy to Respond
If a risk is identified (e.g., a cobalt smelter in your chain is flagged for severe pollution), you cannot simply ignore it. You must implement a mitigation strategy. This ranges from pressuring the supplier to improve, suspending trade while mitigation occurs, or ultimately disengaging if the supplier refuses to comply.

### Step 4: Third-Party Verification
Your due diligence system cannot be self-certified. A **Notified Body** must conduct an independent audit of your supply chain due diligence policies and practices. This audit report is one of the required documents for the regulatory tier of the Battery Passport.

### Step 5: Report Annually
You must publicly report on your due diligence policies and how you managed identified risks. This report must be easily accessible (e.g., linked via the Battery Passport).

## Intersection with the Digital Battery Passport

The Digital Battery Passport is the delivery mechanism for your due diligence data.

While the general public might see a high-level summary of your ESG commitments, the **Regulatory Access Tier** of the passport will hold:
*   The identities of the smelters and refiners in your supply chain.
*   The third-party audit reports of those facilities.
*   Your overarching risk assessment document.

## Industry Initiatives

Most mid-sized economic operators lack the resources to individually audit mines in the DRC or lithium ponds in South America. The regulation permits reliance on recognized **industry schemes** (e.g., the Responsible Minerals Initiative - RMI) to fulfill parts of the due diligence obligations, provided the scheme itself is verified by the EU Commission.

## What to do next

1.  **Supply Chain Mapping:** Immediate priority. If you do not have visibility past your Tier-1 cell supplier, you are behind schedule for the 2025 deadline.
2.  **Contract Revision:** Update your standard procurement contracts to include clauses requiring sub-suppliers to submit to audits and participate in your traceability system.
3.  **Evaluate Traceability Software:** You need a system that can aggregate supplier audit data and attach it to specific battery batches.
