## Overview

When structuring US real estate investments to avoid the 40% [US Estate Tax](/library/us-estate-tax-planning-non-resident-aliens/), the standard advice is to use a "Foreign Blocker" corporation. Historically, this meant setting up a BVI (British Virgin Islands) or Cayman Islands entity.

However, with the maturation of the UAE's corporate landscape, many local investors and family offices are asking: **Can I use a UAE Freezone company (like ADGM, DIFC, or DMCC) as my foreign blocker?**

The short answer is yes, but it requires careful navigation of US tax laws and the specific corporate features of the freezone.

### The Mechanism of the Blocker

To understand if a UAE company works, you must understand what the IRS requires a blocker to be.
For a foreign entity to shield a non-resident alien from the US Estate Tax, the IRS must classify that foreign entity as a **Corporation** for US tax purposes (an "Association Taxable as a Corporation" under the check-the-box regulations).

If the IRS classifies the UAE entity as a partnership or a disregarded entity (like a standard LLC), the estate tax shield fails, and the IRS looks through to the individual owner.

### Qualifying a UAE Entity

Not all UAE entities automatically qualify as corporations under US law. Under IRS Form 8832 ("Entity Classification Election"), a foreign entity must meet certain criteria or make a specific election to be treated as a corporation.

- **Public Joint Stock Companies (PJSC):** Generally classified as corporations automatically (Per Se corporations).
- **Limited Liability Companies (LLC) or Freezone Establishments (FZE):** These are considered "eligible entities." They are *not* automatically corporations. By default, if a single member owns an FZE and that member has limited liability, it might be classified as a disregarded entity. **You must proactively file IRS Form 8832 to elect corporate status.**

### Advantages of Using ADGM or DIFC

1. **Substance and Reputation:** Post-BEPS (Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) and increased global regulatory scrutiny, traditional offshore havens (BVI/Cayman) face heavy compliance friction when opening US bank accounts. ADGM and DIFC entities, operating under English Common Law with robust regulatory frameworks, often pass US banking KYC (Know Your Customer) checks much faster.
2. **Consolidated Wealth Management:** For a Dubai-based family office, keeping the holding structures within the UAE simplifies administration, accounting, and local legal oversight.
3. **Double Taxation Agreements:** While the US-UAE tax treaty does not eliminate US income tax on real estate, having a localized structure ensures any potential treaty benefits regarding dividend repatriation or interest are streamlined.

### The Optimal Structure

1. **Top Level:** UAE Resident / Family Office.
2. **Mid Level (The Blocker):** ADGM or DIFC Private Company (Ltd). *Crucially, Form 8832 is filed with the IRS to elect treatment as a foreign corporation.*
3. **Lower Level (The Holding Company):** US LLC (e.g., Wyoming or Delaware) wholly owned by the ADGM entity.
4. **Asset Level:** The US LLC holds the title to the real estate.

### Important Tax Caveats

While the UAE blocker protects against the Estate Tax, it introduces a different tax: the **Branch Profits Tax (BPT)**.

When a foreign corporation engages in a US trade or business (like operating rental real estate), the US levies a 30% tax on the "dividend equivalent amount" repatriated out of the US to the foreign parent, *in addition* to the standard corporate income tax.

To avoid the Branch Profits Tax, investors often introduce debt into the structure (Portfolio Interest Exemption) or ensure the US LLC is taxed as a US C-Corporation, which pays the corporate tax rate (21%) and then a dividend withholding tax, rather than the BPT.
