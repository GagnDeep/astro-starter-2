# Raw Vs Cooked Food Calories

Tracking your nutrition effectively is the cornerstone of any successful diet, whether your goal is fat loss, muscle gain, or maintaining your current physique.

## Why This Matters

When it comes to nutrition, precision matters. Many people struggle to see results not because they lack discipline, but because they are inadvertently miscalculating their intake.

*According to the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, individuals tend to underestimate their caloric intake by up to 30%.*

### Common Mistakes

1. **Not using a food scale:** Volume measurements (like cups or tablespoons) are notoriously inaccurate. A "tablespoon" of peanut butter can vary wildly depending on who is scooping it. Always weigh your food in grams.
2. **Forgetting liquid calories:** Oils, dressings, sodas, and alcohol often go untracked but can easily push you out of a calorie deficit.
3. **Misunderstanding raw vs. cooked weights:** Meat loses weight when cooked (due to water loss), while grains like rice and pasta gain weight (by absorbing water). Always track the state in which you weighed the food.

## Worked Example

Let's look at a common scenario: tracking a chicken breast.

If you weigh 150g of raw chicken breast, it contains approximately 165 calories and 31g of protein. If you cook that same piece of chicken, it might weigh 110g after water loss. If you mistakenly log the 110g as *raw* chicken, you will under-report your intake by about 45 calories.

| Food Item | Raw Weight | Cooked Weight | Calories | Protein |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Chicken Breast | 150g | ~110g | 165 kcal | 31g |
| White Rice | 50g | ~150g | 180 kcal | 4g |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to track macros forever?**
No. Tracking is a tool to educate yourself about portion sizes and the nutritional density of foods. Once you have a good understanding, you can transition to intuitive eating.

**How accurate are food labels?**
The FDA allows for a 20% margin of error on nutrition labels. This is why consistency is more important than absolute perfection.
