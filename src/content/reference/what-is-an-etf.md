An Exchange Traded Fund (ETF) is a basket of securities—like stocks or bonds—that tracks an underlying index, such as the S&P 500.

Unlike mutual funds, which are priced once at the end of the trading day, ETFs are traded on a stock exchange just like individual stocks. You can buy and sell them throughout the trading day at the current market price.

### Why are ETFs popular?
1. **Diversification:** Buying one share of an S&P 500 ETF gives you tiny pieces of 500 different companies.
2. **Low Cost:** Because most ETFs simply track an index automatically (passive management), they charge very low fees (expense ratios) compared to actively managed mutual funds.
3. **Tax Efficiency:** The structure of ETFs makes them generally more tax-efficient than mutual funds.
