# W2 Form Guide

The Form W-2, Wage and Tax Statement, is the document your employer sends you (and the IRS) at the end of the year. If this is your only income, you should **never** pay to file your taxes.

## Overview
Your W-2 shows your total earnings, as well as the amount of federal, state, and other taxes withheld from your paycheck.

### Key Boxes Explained
- **Box 1 (Wages, tips, other comp):** Your taxable income. This is usually lower than your gross salary because of pre-tax deductions like health insurance or 401(k) contributions.
- **Box 2 (Federal income tax withheld):** The amount of money you already paid to the government. This is the crucial number for your [Tax Refund Calculator](/tools/tax-refund-calculator/).
- **Box 3 & 4 (Social Security):** Wages subject to Social Security tax, and the tax withheld.
- **Box 5 & 6 (Medicare):** Wages subject to Medicare tax, and the tax withheld.
- **Box 12:** Codes for various compensations/deductions (e.g., Code D for 401k, Code DD for employer-sponsored health coverage).

### Worked Example: Why "Free" Software Works for W-2s
Emma earned $65,000 (Box 1) and had $6,000 withheld (Box 2).
Because her tax situation is solely W-2 income, every major tax software's "Free Edition" will accept her return. She takes the [Standard Deduction](/guides/standard-deduction-2024/) and files her federal taxes in 15 minutes.
*Note:* While federal is free, she must check if the software charges $40 to file her [State Income Taxes](/guides/state-income-tax-rates/).

### Edge Cases
- **Missing W-2:** Employers must send W-2s by January 31. If you don't receive it by mid-February, contact your employer. If they refuse, you can use Form 4852 (Substitute for Form W-2).
- **Multiple W-2s:** Having multiple W-2s from changing jobs doesn't complicate your taxes—you simply add the Box 1 and Box 2 amounts together. However, some paid software attempts to charge you if you have more than one W-2. Read our post on [Filing Taxes with Multiple W-2s](/blog/filing-multiple-w2s/).

### Common Mistakes
- **Typing errors:** Putting a decimal in the wrong place can delay your refund by weeks. Always double-check your EIN (Employer Identification Number).
- **Ignoring Box 12:** Misunderstanding the codes can lead to panic. (e.g., Code DD is informational and not taxable).
