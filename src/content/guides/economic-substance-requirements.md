The Cayman Islands International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act (ES Act) was introduced in 2019 to ensure that entities registered in the Cayman Islands that carry out certain "relevant activities" have demonstrable economic substance in the jurisdiction.

## What Entities are in Scope?

The law applies to "relevant entities," which generally include:
*   Cayman Islands exempted companies, ordinary companies, and LLCs.
*   Foreign companies registered in the Cayman Islands.
*   Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs).

Investment funds are expressly excluded from the definition of a relevant entity, but their subsidiaries may still fall in scope.

## The 9 Relevant Activities

An entity must meet the economic substance test if it conducts any of the following:

1.  Banking business
2.  Distribution and service centre business
3.  Financing and leasing business
4.  Fund management business
5.  Headquarters business
6.  Holding company business (subject to a reduced test)
7.  Insurance business
8.  Intellectual property business (subject to high-risk requirements)
9.  Shipping business

## The Economic Substance Test

If a relevant entity conducts a relevant activity, it must satisfy the Economic Substance Test. Generally, this requires the entity to:

1.  Conduct **Cayman Islands Core Income Generating Activities (CIGA)** in relation to the relevant activity.
2.  Be **directed and managed** in an appropriate manner in the Cayman Islands.
3.  Have an **adequate amount of operating expenditure** incurred in the Cayman Islands.
4.  Have an **adequate physical presence** (including maintaining a place of business or plant, property and equipment) in the Islands.
5.  Have an **adequate number of full-time employees** or other personnel with appropriate qualifications in the Islands.

## Reporting and Compliance

Every relevant entity must notify the Cayman Islands Tax Information Authority (TIA) annually, stating whether or not it is carrying on a relevant activity. Entities conducting a relevant activity must then file a comprehensive Economic Substance Return within 12 months of the end of their financial year.

Penalties for non-compliance are severe, starting at CI$10,000 (approx. US$12,000) and escalating rapidly, potentially leading to the entity being struck off the register.
