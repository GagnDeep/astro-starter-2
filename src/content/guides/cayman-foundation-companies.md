The **Foundation Companies Act** was introduced in the Cayman Islands to create a highly flexible, hybrid corporate vehicle that blends the characteristics of a standard limited liability company with those of a common law trust.

Today, Foundation Companies (FCs) are the vehicle of choice for Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs), Web3 projects, and high-net-worth families looking for an alternative to traditional trusts.

## Key Characteristics of a Foundation Company

1.  **Legal Personality:** Unlike a trust (which is a legal relationship), an FC has separate legal personality. It can hold assets, sue, and be sued in its own name.
2.  **No Shareholders Required:** An FC can be structured to have no members (shareholders) after incorporation. It exists to fulfill its stated objects, which can be for a specific purpose or for the benefit of beneficiaries.
3.  **Limited Liability:** It is incorporated as an Exempted Company, offering the standard protections of limited liability.
4.  **Bylaws:** Alongside its Memorandum and Articles of Association (M&A), an FC is governed by internal, private Bylaws that do not need to be filed publicly.

## Primary Use Cases

### 1. Web3, DAOs, and Token Issuance
DAOs face a critical problem: without a legal wrapper, they operate as general partnerships, meaning participants could have unlimited personal liability. A Cayman Foundation Company provides a "legal wrapper" for the DAO.
*   **The Structure:** The FC is established with no shareholders. It is directed by Supervisors or a Board of Directors who are contractually bound (via the Bylaws) to execute the will of the DAO token holders.
*   **Token Issuance:** The FC can issue grants, hold treasury assets, and interface with the traditional fiat world (e.g., signing contracts, hiring developers) on behalf of the DAO.

### 2. Private Wealth and Succession Planning
Families from civil law jurisdictions often struggle with the concept of a common law trust, as it requires transferring legal title of assets to a third-party trustee.
*   **The Alternative:** An FC allows the family to retain control via a Board of Directors, while the entity itself holds the assets for the benefit of future generations. It provides the same asset protection and succession benefits as a trust, but in a familiar corporate format.

## Mandatory Roles in a Foundation Company

To maintain its status under the Act, an FC must have:

*   **Founder:** The person who establishes the FC and contributes the initial assets. The Founder can reserve significant rights for themselves in the Bylaws (e.g., the right to appoint/remove directors).
*   **Directors:** Must have a board of directors (at least one) to manage the day-to-day affairs.
*   **Supervisor:** Because an FC may have no shareholders to hold the directors accountable, the law requires the appointment of a Supervisor. The Supervisor enforces the rules of the FC against the directors. (The Founder can also be the Supervisor).
*   **Secretary:** An FC must have a "Qualified Person" as its secretary, which by law means a licensed Cayman Islands trust company or corporate service provider. The Secretary acts as a regulatory gatekeeper to ensure AML/CFT compliance.

## The Incorporation Process

1.  **Drafting:** The M&A and Bylaws must be carefully drafted by Cayman counsel. The M&A must explicitly state the company is a Foundation Company and that it is prohibited from paying dividends to its members (if it has any).
2.  **Service Provider:** Engage a licensed Cayman service provider to act as the Qualified Secretary.
3.  **Filing:** File the M&A with the Registrar of Companies and pay the incorporation fee.

## Comparison: Foundation Company vs. Trust

| Feature | Foundation Company | Cayman Trust |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Status** | Separate Legal Entity | Fiduciary Relationship |
| **Control** | Board of Directors / Supervisors | Trustee |
| **Asset Ownership** | Owned by the FC itself | Legal title held by Trustee |
| **Beneficiary Rights** | Can be severely restricted via Bylaws | High rights to information and enforcement |
| **Setup Cost** | Generally higher (incorporation + drafting) | Generally lower (trust deed execution) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are Foundation Companies subject to Economic Substance?**
Yes. An FC must file an annual Economic Substance notification. If it conducts a "relevant activity" (e.g., acting as a holding company or conducting financing/leasing), it must demonstrate substance on the island.

**Can an FC carry out commercial trading?**
Yes, but only if that trading is incidental or necessary to achieving its primary objects. It cannot operate purely as a standard trading business.

**Do we have to disclose the beneficiaries?**
The Bylaws and the identities of the beneficiaries are private documents and are not filed with the Registrar of Companies. However, the Qualified Secretary must maintain a register of supervisors and know the beneficiaries for KYC/AML purposes.
