Historically, closed-ended funds (where investors do not have the right to redeem their shares at their own option) were largely exempt from regulation by the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA). This changed dramatically with the introduction of the **Private Funds Act (PFA)** in 2020.

Today, almost all Private Equity, Venture Capital, and Real Estate funds structured in the Cayman Islands must register with CIMA.

## What Constitutes a Private Fund?

Under the PFA, a Private Fund is any company, unit trust, or partnership that offers or issues investment interests, the purpose or effect of which is the pooling of investor funds with the aim of enabling investors to receive profits or gains from the entity's acquisition, holding, management, or disposal of investments.

Crucially, to be a Private Fund:
1.  The investors must not have day-to-day control over the investments.
2.  The investments must be managed as a whole by or on behalf of the operator of the fund.
3.  The fund must be closed-ended (no voluntary redemption right for investors).

## Exclusions
Single-investor funds are excluded (as there is no pooling). Securitization special purpose vehicles (SPVs), joint ventures, and proprietary vehicles are also carved out.

## The Registration Process

A Private Fund must submit its registration application to CIMA within 21 days after accepting capital commitments from investors, but crucially, **before it accepts capital contributions for the purposes of investment**.

**Required Filings via REEFS Portal:**
*   Application form and application fee (CI$300) plus initial registration fee (CI$3,500).
*   Certificate of Incorporation/Registration.
*   Constitutional documents (e.g., LPA).
*   Offering Document, Summary of Terms, or Marketing Pitchbook.
*   Auditor consent letter and Administrator consent letter (if applicable).

## The Big Four Operating Obligations

Once registered, a Private Fund must comply with four core operational requirements designed to protect investors.

### 1. Annual Audit
The fund must have its accounts audited annually by a CIMA-approved Cayman Islands auditor. The audited financials must be filed within six months of the financial year-end, along with a Fund Annual Return (FAR) form.

### 2. Valuation of Assets
The fund must have appropriate and consistent procedures for the proper valuation of its assets. Valuations must be carried out at least once a year.
*   **Who can do it:** Valuations can be done by an independent third party, the fund's administrator, or the investment manager itself (provided the valuation function is strictly segregated from the portfolio management function to manage conflicts of interest).

### 3. Safekeeping of Fund Assets
The fund must appoint a custodian to hold the custodial assets in segregated accounts.
*   **Exception:** For Private Equity funds where it is impractical or disproportionate to appoint a custodian (e.g., holding shares in private companies rather than tradable securities), the fund must notify CIMA and ensure a designated person performs "title verification" to verify that the fund actually owns the assets it claims to own.

### 4. Cash Monitoring
The fund must appoint someone to monitor its cash flows, ensure that all cash has been booked in cash accounts opened in the name of the fund, and ensure that all payments made by investors have been received.

## Alternative Investment Vehicles (AIVs)
Many PE structures use AIVs for specific tax or regulatory structuring. If a Private Fund utilizes an AIV, the AIV itself usually does not need to separately register with CIMA, provided its financial information is consolidated or incorporated into the registered Private Fund's audited financials and FAR filing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do Private Funds need to appoint independent directors?**
Unlike Mutual Funds (hedge funds), a Private Fund structured as an Exempted Limited Partnership (the vast majority) does not explicitly require independent directors on the board of its General Partner. However, standard fiduciary duties still apply.

**Does the DRLA apply to Private Funds?**
Yes. If the Private Fund is a corporate entity, its directors must register under the Directors Registration and Licensing Act. If it is a partnership, the directors of its corporate General Partner must register.

**What is the penalty for not registering?**
Failure to register a Private Fund before drawing down capital for investments constitutes an offense, liable on conviction to a fine of CI$100,000.
