The **Segregated Portfolio Company (SPC)** is a specialized type of Cayman Islands Exempted Company. It allows a single legal entity to create distinct, internal "portfolios" that legally separate the assets and liabilities of one portfolio from those of the others.

## The Core Concept: Statutory Ring-Fencing

The defining feature of an SPC is statutory segregation. Under the Companies Act, if a creditor has a claim against "Portfolio A," that creditor can only seize the assets held within "Portfolio A."

The creditor has **no legal right** to claim against the assets of "Portfolio B" or against the general assets of the SPC itself. This internal ring-fencing is created by statute, avoiding the need to set up dozens of separate subsidiary companies.

## How an SPC Operates

1.  **Single Legal Entity:** The SPC itself is the only legal entity. The individual segregated portfolios are *not* separate legal entities. When a contract is signed, it is signed by the SPC acting "for and on behalf of Portfolio A."
2.  **General Assets vs. Portfolio Assets:** The SPC must keep the assets of each portfolio separately identifiable. Assets that are not allocated to a specific portfolio form the "General Assets" of the SPC.
3.  **Cross-Class Liability:** While the statute protects the portfolios from each other, it is standard practice to reinforce this by including "limited recourse" wording in all contracts signed by the SPC, ensuring counterparties acknowledge they can only target a specific portfolio.

## Primary Use Cases

### 1. "Umbrella" Investment Funds
An investment manager may want to run several different investment strategies (e.g., a Tech Fund, a Real Estate Fund, and a Crypto Fund) without the massive overhead of incorporating three separate fund companies, paying three sets of CIMA registration fees, and appointing three boards of directors.
*   By using an SPC, the manager creates one master entity. Portfolio A holds the tech assets, Portfolio B the real estate, etc. Investors buy shares specifically linked to one portfolio. If the Crypto Fund collapses, the Tech Fund's assets are protected by statute.

### 2. Captive Insurance
The SPC structure was originally designed for the insurance industry. It allows a captive insurance manager to host multiple, unrelated insurance programs (e.g., for different corporate clients) within a single legal entity (often called a "Rent-a-Captive" structure), ensuring the claims of one client's insurance pool do not wipe out the reserves of another.

### 3. Multi-Family Offices
Wealth managers use SPCs to manage the assets of different high-net-worth families within one vehicle, customizing the investment strategy for each family via their designated portfolio, while sharing the administrative costs of the core SPC.

## Administrative Requirements

Running an SPC requires meticulous administration.
*   **Name:** The company's name must include "SPC" or "Segregated Portfolio Company."
*   **Contracting:** Directors must ensure that every contract clearly identifies which specific segregated portfolio the SPC is acting on behalf of. Failing to do so can result in the directors being held personally liable.
*   **Accounting:** The administrator must maintain entirely separate accounting records for each portfolio.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the SPC structure recognized outside the Cayman Islands?**
Yes. While the concept originated offshore, segregated portfolio (or protected cell) legislation has been tested and respected in major onshore courts, including the UK and the US, provided the internal accounting segregation was properly maintained.

**Can a portfolio contract with another portfolio in the same SPC?**
Because the portfolios are not separate legal entities, they cannot legally contract with each other (a company cannot sign a contract with itself). However, the Companies Act provides a specific mechanism allowing assets to be transferred between portfolios at fair value.

**What happens if an SPC goes into liquidation?**
If a single portfolio is insolvent, the court can issue a "Receivership Order" specifically for that portfolio, winding it down without affecting the solvent portfolios or putting the entire SPC into liquidation.
