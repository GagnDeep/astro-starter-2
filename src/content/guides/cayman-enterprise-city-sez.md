Cayman Enterprise City (CEC) is the Cayman Islands' award-winning Special Economic Zone (SEZ). It was designed specifically to attract knowledge-based industries—such as technology, media, marketing, commodities trading, and aviation—to set up a physical presence in the jurisdiction.

For foreign entrepreneurs and tech startups, CEC is often the fastest and most efficient way to establish a physical operating company on the island.

## Key Benefits of the SEZ

1.  **100% Foreign Ownership:** Unlike a standard local Ordinary Company (which requires 60% Caymanian ownership under the Local Companies Control Act), a CEC company can be 100% foreign-owned.
2.  **No Corporate Tax:** As with all Cayman entities, there is no corporate tax, income tax, or capital gains tax.
3.  **Fast-Track Work Permits:** This is often the biggest draw. Work permits for CEC companies (called Zone Employment Certificates, or ZECs) are processed within 5 working days. They also bypass the local advertising requirement (JobsCayman portal) that standard work permits require.
4.  **Turnkey Office Space:** CEC provides fully serviced co-working spaces or dedicated private offices, satisfying Economic Substance physical presence requirements immediately.

## Who Qualifies?

CEC is divided into several "parks." To qualify, your business must fall into one of these categories and demonstrate that its revenue is generated primarily from outside the Cayman Islands.

*   **Cayman Tech City:** For software developers, IT services, blockchain, Web3, and fintech companies.
*   **Cayman Commodities & Derivatives City:** For proprietary trading, investment management, and commodities firms.
*   **Cayman Maritime & Aviation City:** For shipping and aviation logistics and management.
*   **Cayman Media Park:** For digital marketing, broadcasting, and publishing.

*Note: Retail, local construction, and traditional local services (law firms, local accounting) are not permitted in the SEZ.*

## The Setup Process

1.  **Application:** Submit a business plan to the CEC team to ensure your business model qualifies for one of the parks.
2.  **Incorporation:** CEC acts as your corporate service provider. They will incorporate an Exempted Company for you (which is legally structured as an SEZ Company).
3.  **SEZ Trade Certificate:** Once incorporated, the Special Economic Zone Authority (SEZA) issues your Trade Certificate, which acts as your business license.
4.  **Office and Visas:** Sign your lease agreement for CEC office space and immediately submit applications for Zone Employment Certificates for your staff.

The entire process, from initial application to having staff on the ground with valid visas, can be completed in 4 to 6 weeks.

## Cost Structure

Operating in CEC is a premium service. Costs are bundled into an annual fee that includes the Trade Certificate, corporate registered office fees, and the physical office lease (which ranges from a shared desk to a private suite).

Expect minimum annual commitments to start around US$18,000 to US$25,000 for a one-person setup (including one ZEC and a shared desk), scaling up significantly for dedicated private offices and multiple staff members.

## Economic Substance

Setting up in CEC is a very robust way to satisfy the Cayman Islands Economic Substance requirements. By having physical office space and full-time employees holding ZECs on the island, a CEC company can easily demonstrate that it conducts its Core Income Generating Activities (CIGA) within the jurisdiction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a CEC company do business with local Caymanians?**
Generally, no. A CEC company cannot compete in the domestic market. However, there are exceptions for B2B services if the CEC company provides a specialized tech service that is not readily available locally, though this requires specific permission.

**Do ZECs count towards Permanent Residency?**
Yes. Time spent in the Cayman Islands on a Zone Employment Certificate counts towards the 8-year residency requirement to apply for Permanent Residency (PR).

**Is CEC regulated by CIMA?**
The SEZ Authority regulates the zone itself. However, if your business conducts financial activities (e.g., you are a Virtual Asset Service Provider or an Investment Fund), you must *also* register with the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) in addition to your CEC licensing.
