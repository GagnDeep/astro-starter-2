While the Cayman Islands levies no direct corporate or personal income taxes, it is fully integrated into the global tax transparency framework. The entity responsible for enforcing this transparency is the **Tax Information Authority (TIA)**, which operates under the Department for International Tax Cooperation (DITC).

## What is the TIA?

The TIA is the sole channel in the Cayman Islands for the provision of tax-related information to other governments. Its primary mandate is to ensure that the Cayman Islands complies with its international obligations regarding the exchange of tax information.

## Core Functions of the TIA

### 1. Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI)
The TIA is the central hub for AEOI in the Cayman Islands, managing two massive global reporting frameworks:
*   **FATCA (US Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act):** Cayman Financial Institutions must report information on US account holders to the TIA, which automatically transmits this data to the IRS.
*   **CRS (Common Reporting Standard):** The OECD equivalent to FATCA. Cayman Financial Institutions must report information on account holders tax-resident in over 100 partner jurisdictions. The TIA then securely exchanges this data with the respective foreign tax authorities.

**The DITC Portal:** Financial Institutions must register with the TIA via the DITC Portal and submit their FATCA/CRS XML returns annually by July 31st.

### 2. Economic Substance Reporting
The TIA is responsible for enforcing the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act. All relevant entities must file an annual Economic Substance Notification. Those conducting a "relevant activity" must file a detailed Economic Substance Return via the DITC Portal, demonstrating they have adequate physical presence and operating expenditure on the island.
*   If an entity fails the ES test, the TIA will levy fines and is legally obligated to spontaneously exchange information regarding the failure with the tax authorities where the entity's immediate parent, ultimate parent, and ultimate beneficial owners reside.

### 3. Country-by-Country (CbC) Reporting
Under the OECD's BEPS (Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) Action 13, Multinational Enterprise (MNE) Groups with consolidated revenue exceeding US$850 million must file a CbC Report. If the ultimate parent entity is resident in the Cayman Islands, the report is filed with the TIA.

### 4. Exchange of Information on Request (EOIR)
The Cayman Islands has signed dozens of Tax Information Exchange Agreements (TIEAs) and is a party to the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters. If a foreign tax authority (like the UK's HMRC or the US IRS) is investigating a specific taxpayer for tax evasion and believes relevant records are held in Cayman, they can make a formal request to the TIA. The TIA has the legal power to compel Cayman banks, corporate service providers, and individuals to produce those records, overriding standard confidentiality laws.

## Compliance and Enforcement

The TIA has significant enforcement powers. Over the past few years, the DITC has shifted from an educational approach to an aggressive enforcement approach.
*   **Administrative Fines:** The TIA regularly issues fines for late FATCA/CRS filings, inaccurate reporting, and failure to meet Economic Substance requirements. Fines start at CI$5,000 and can escalate rapidly.
*   **Audits:** The TIA conducts proactive compliance audits on Cayman Financial Institutions to ensure their AEOI reporting procedures are robust.
