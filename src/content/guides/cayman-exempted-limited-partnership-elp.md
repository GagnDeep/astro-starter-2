The **Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP)** is the dominant legal structure used for Private Equity, Venture Capital, and Real Estate funds in the Cayman Islands. Its design closely mirrors the Delaware Limited Partnership, making it highly familiar to US sponsors and institutional investors, while offering the tax neutrality and flexibility of an offshore jurisdiction.

## Legal Concept: No Separate Legal Personality

The most fundamental characteristic of a Cayman ELP is that, unlike an Exempted Company, an **ELP does not have a separate legal personality distinct from its partners**.

Because it is not a separate legal entity:
*   The ELP cannot own property in its own name.
*   The ELP cannot sue or be sued in its own name.
*   Instead, all assets are held, and all contracts are entered into, by the **General Partner (GP)** on trust for the ELP and its limited partners.

## The Roles: General Partner vs. Limited Partner

An ELP must have at least one General Partner and at least one Limited Partner.

### 1. The General Partner (GP)
The GP is responsible for the management and conduct of the ELP's business.
*   **Liability:** The GP has unlimited liability for all the debts and obligations of the ELP in the event that the ELP's assets are insufficient.
*   **Structuring:** To protect individuals from this unlimited liability, the GP is almost always a limited liability company (e.g., a Cayman Exempted Company or a Delaware LLC).
*   **Registration:** At least one GP must be a Cayman Islands resident company, a registered foreign company in Cayman, or another Cayman ELP.

### 2. The Limited Partners (LPs)
The LPs are the investors. They contribute capital to the ELP but do not participate in its day-to-day management.
*   **Liability:** An LP's liability is strictly limited to the amount of their capital commitment, provided they do not take part in the "conduct of the business."
*   **Safe Harbors:** The ELP Act includes a robust list of "safe harbors"—activities an LP can undertake without losing their limited liability shield. This includes serving on a Limited Partner Advisory Committee (LPAC), consulting with the GP, or voting on the removal of the GP.

## Formation and Documentation

Forming an ELP is straightforward and relies heavily on freedom of contract.
1.  **Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA):** This is the core governing document. It dictates the economic terms (waterfalls, carry, management fees), voting rights, and the powers of the GP. It is a private contract and is not filed with the government.
2.  **Section 9 Statement:** To register the ELP, the GP files a short statement with the Registrar of Exempted Limited Partnerships detailing the ELP's name, general nature of business, registered office, and the GP's details.

## Why is it used for Private Equity?

1.  **Tax Transparency:** Because the ELP is not a separate legal entity, it is "tax transparent" or a "pass-through" vehicle. Profits and losses flow directly to the LPs, who are taxed in their home jurisdictions. This is crucial for avoiding double taxation.
2.  **Familiarity:** The Cayman ELP Act was drafted specifically to align with the Delaware Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act. US lawyers and investors understand exactly how it works.
3.  **Flexibility:** The Act allows the partners immense freedom to negotiate the terms of their commercial relationship in the LPA, overriding many default statutory rules that apply to companies.

## Regulatory Considerations

*   **Private Funds Act:** If the ELP is operating as a closed-ended investment fund, it must register with CIMA under the Private Funds Act before accepting capital contributions for investment.
*   **Economic Substance:** An ELP that acts as a "Fund" is currently outside the scope of the Economic Substance Act. However, if the ELP conducts other relevant activities (e.g., financing and leasing), it may be in scope.
*   **AML/CFT:** An ELP operating as a fund is a "Financial Institution" and must comply with Cayman AML Regulations, including appointing an AMLCO and MLRO.
