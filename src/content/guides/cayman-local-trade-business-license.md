While the Cayman Islands is famous for its offshore Exempted Companies, operating a business physically on the island (e.g., a retail store, a local consulting firm, a restaurant) requires an entirely different setup. You must obtain a **Trade and Business License (TBL)** from the Department of Commerce and Investment (DCI).

## The Local Company Structure (Ordinary Company)

To operate locally, you generally form an Ordinary Resident Company. Unlike an Exempted Company, an Ordinary Company is required to keep a Register of Members that is open to public inspection at its registered office, and it must hold an Annual General Meeting.

## The 60/40 Rule (Local Control Law)

The most significant hurdle for expats wanting to start a local business is the **Local Companies (Control) Act (LCCA)**. This law dictates that an Ordinary Company carrying on business in the Cayman Islands must be at least **60% beneficially owned by Caymanians**, and at least 60% of its directors must be Caymanian.

### How Expats Navigate the 60/40 Rule

If a non-Caymanian wishes to own more than 40% of a local business, they have two main options:

1.  **LCCL License:** The company can apply for a Local Companies (Control) License. The Trade and Business Licensing Board will only grant this if the applicant proves that no Caymanian was willing or able to partner in the business (a process requiring public advertising). It is difficult and time-consuming to obtain, though exceptions are sometimes made for highly specialized businesses that bring significant economic value or employment opportunities to the islands.
2.  **Special Economic Zone (SEZ):** For tech, media, commodities, and certain intellectual property businesses, setting up in Cayman Enterprise City (the SEZ) bypasses the 60/40 rule entirely. SEZ companies can be 100% foreign-owned and still operate from within the Cayman Islands, provided their revenue is generated primarily internationally.

## The TBL Application Process

If you meet the ownership requirements (or have your LCCL/SEZ setup), you must apply to the DCI for the TBL.

**Required Documentation:**
1.  **Completed Application Form:** Detailing the exact nature of the business.
2.  **Police Clearances:** Required for all directors and beneficial owners.
3.  **Bank Reference Letters:** For all non-Caymanian directors/owners.
4.  **Proof of Premises:** A signed lease agreement or letter of intent. The DCI requires commercial premises for most businesses; you cannot easily register a local trading business to a residential P.O. Box.
5.  **Approvals:** Depending on the business, you may need sign-offs from the Department of Environmental Health (for food), the Fire Service, or the Planning Department.

## Fees

The cost of a TBL varies wildly depending on the business category and location (George Town vs. Sister Islands).
*   A small consulting firm might pay CI$500 annually.
*   A large retail store or financial services firm (that isn't CIMA regulated) might pay several thousand dollars.

## Penalties

Operating a local business without a valid TBL, or operating outside the scope of your licensed category, is a criminal offense. The DCI conducts regular inspections, particularly in the retail and hospitality sectors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can an Exempted Company get a TBL?**
Generally, no. Exempted Companies are prohibited by law from trading locally in the Cayman Islands (except in furtherance of their offshore business). To trade locally, an Exempted Company would need to apply to be deregistered as an Exempted Company and re-registered as an Ordinary Company, or set up a local subsidiary subject to the 60/40 rule.

**Does a TBL give me the right to work?**
No. Holding shares in a local company or being a director on a TBL application does not grant an expat the right to work on the island. You must still apply for and be granted a Work Permit by WORC to actively manage or work in the business.

**Do I need a TBL if I just rent out my condo on Airbnb?**
Yes. Following recent regulatory changes, short-term rentals and Airbnb operations require a Trade and Business License, as well as registration with the Department of Tourism.
