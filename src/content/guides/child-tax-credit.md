# Child Tax Credit (2024 Updates)

Understanding the Child Tax Credit (CTC) is crucial for families. Software companies frequently try to upcharge families claiming this credit, even though many qualify for free filing.

## Overview
The Child Tax Credit provides a significant tax break to taxpayers who have qualifying children under the age of 17.

### Key Facts and Figures (2024)
- **Maximum Credit:** $2,000 per qualifying child.
- **Refundable Portion (ACTC):** Up to $1,700 per child is refundable (meaning you can get it back even if you owe $0 in tax).
- **Phase-out Threshold:** The credit begins to phase out if your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) is over $200,000 (Single/HOH) or $400,000 (Married Filing Jointly).

*Source: IRS guidelines for Tax Year 2024.*

### Worked Example
Mark and Lisa are married, filing jointly, with an AGI of $80,000 and two children (ages 8 and 10).
- **Total Potential Credit:** 2 children × $2,000 = $4,000.
- Since their AGI is well below the $400,000 phase-out limit, they get the full $4,000 credit.
- This $4,000 directly reduces their [Tax Liability](/glossary/tax-liability/) dollar-for-dollar. If their tax liability drops below zero, up to $3,400 (2 × $1,700) can be refunded to them.

Because their income is $80,000, they might just miss the [IRS Free File](/guides/irs-free-file/) cutoff, but they can still use free options like Cash App Taxes (see our [Cash App Taxes Review](/blog/cash-app-taxes-review)).

### Edge Cases
- **Age limit:** The child must be *under 17* at the end of the year. A 17-year-old does not qualify for the CTC, though they might qualify for the $500 Credit for Other Dependents.
- **Divorced Parents:** Only one parent can claim the child. This is usually the custodial parent, unless a Form 8332 is signed.

### Common Mistakes
- **Paying for Software:** Upgrading to "Premium" software just to file Schedule 8812 (the form for the CTC). Truly free software includes this form.
- **Confusing it with the Child and Dependent Care Credit:** The CTC is for having a child; the other is for *daycare expenses*.
