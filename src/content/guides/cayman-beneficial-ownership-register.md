The Cayman Islands has recently overhauled its beneficial ownership regime with the introduction of the **Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act (BOTA)**. This legislation consolidates previous laws and expands the scope of entities required to maintain and file beneficial ownership information.

## What is a Beneficial Owner?

A Beneficial Owner (UBO) is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls a legal entity. Under BOTA, a person is a beneficial owner if they meet any of the following criteria:

1.  They hold, directly or indirectly, **25% or more** of the shares or voting rights in the company.
2.  They have the right, directly or indirectly, to appoint or remove a majority of the board of directors.
3.  If no individual meets criteria 1 or 2, the beneficial owner is the person who exercises "ultimate effective control" over the entity through other means.
4.  If absolutely no individual can be identified under the first three criteria, the law requires a "Senior Managing Official" (e.g., a CEO or professional director) to be identified as the contact person.

## Who is In Scope?

BOTA significantly expands the regime. It applies to almost all entities registered in the Cayman Islands, including:
*   Exempted Companies, Ordinary Companies, and LLCs.
*   Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs).
*   Foundation Companies.
*   *Crucial Update:* Exempted Limited Partnerships (ELPs) are now expected to fall into scope under the new framework.

**Exemptions:** The list of exemptions has been severely restricted. Previously, investment funds regulated by CIMA were entirely exempt from maintaining a UBO register. Under BOTA, this broad exemption is removed. Regulated funds must now either maintain a register or utilize an alternative route (which generally involves providing CIMA with the contact details of a licensed administrator who holds the UBO data).

## How the Register Works

Entities do not file this information directly with the government. The process works as follows:
1.  The entity must identify its UBOs and provide their details (name, date of birth, nationality, residential address, passport number, and nature of control) to its Corporate Service Provider (CSP) in the Cayman Islands.
2.  The CSP maintains the entity's Beneficial Ownership Register.
3.  The CSP securely uploads this data to the centralized search platform operated by the Cayman Islands Government (the Competent Authority).

## Is the Register Public?

**No.** Currently, the Cayman Islands beneficial ownership register is **not open to the general public**.

However, the centralized platform can be searched instantly by competent authorities, including:
*   The Royal Cayman Islands Police Service (RCIPS) and the Financial Reporting Authority (FRA).
*   The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) and the Tax Information Authority (TIA).
*   The UK National Crime Agency (NCA) – via a specific agreement allowing rapid access for UK law enforcement.

*Note on Public Registers:* The Cayman Islands government has committed to implementing public registers of beneficial ownership, but only when such registers become a globally accepted standard (following recent European Court of Justice rulings that pushed back on the legality of public registers based on privacy rights).

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failing to maintain a register, failing to keep it updated, or providing false information are criminal offenses. Both the company and the individual beneficial owners who fail to provide their information can face significant fines and potential imprisonment.
