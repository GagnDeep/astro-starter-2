## Welcome to the 1099 Reality Check

When you quit a $90,000 W-2 job and land your first $90,000 freelance contract, you might feel like you earned a 1:1 income swap.

Then tax season hits, and you discover the **15.3% Self-Employment Tax shock**.

Understanding how 1099 income differs from W-2 wage income is the single most important financial concept for creators, contractors, and solo founders.

---

## The Big Difference: FICA & Employer Taxes

When you work as a W-2 employee:
- Your employer pays **7.65%** in payroll taxes (6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare) behind the scenes.
- You pay **7.65%** withheld automatically from your paycheck.

When you become a 1099 independent contractor:
- You are both the employee **AND** the employer.
- You must pay the combined rate: **15.3% Self-Employment Tax** (12.4% Social Security up to wage caps + 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of your net business profit.

---

## How 1099 Income Is Actually Calculated

```
Gross 1099 Income
  minus Business Write-Offs (Laptops, Software, Office, Mileage)
  =============================================================
  = Net Business Profit (Schedule C)

Net Business Profit × 92.35%
  × 15.3% Self-Employment Tax Rate
  =============================================================
  = Self-Employment Tax Owed
```

### The 1099 Tax Shield Advantage: Write-Offs
W-2 employees cannot write off their laptops, home Wi-Fi, or software. 1099 contractors can! Every valid dollar of business expense lowers your net profit **before** SE tax and income tax are calculated.

## 1099 vs W-2 Take-Home Pay Comparison ($100,000 Income)

| Metric / Tax Line | W-2 Employee ($100k Salary) | 1099 Contractor ($100k Gross - $15k Expenses = $85k Net) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gross Earnings** | $100,000 | $100,000 |
| **Business Deductions** | $0 | **-$15,000** |
| **Taxable Net Profit** | $100,000 | **$85,000** |
| **FICA / SE Tax Owed** | $7,650 | **$12,010** *(50% deductible on Form 1040)* |
| **Standard Deduction** | -$15,000 | -$15,000 |
| **QBI Deduction (Sec 199A)**| $0 | **-$14,000** *(20% off taxable profit)* |
| **Estimated Total Federal Tax**| ~$14,200 | **~$13,850** |
| **Approx. Effective Tax Rate** | 14.2% | **13.85%** |

*Notice:* Even with the 15.3% SE tax, taking valid business write-offs and the 20% Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction often leads to a **lower total tax burden** for smart 1099 freelancers than W-2 employees!

## 3 Rules to Survive as a 1099 Freelancer

1. **Set Aside 25%–30% of Every Client Payment:** The moment a client pays an invoice, transfer 25%–30% into a dedicated tax savings account. Do not touch this money for rent or personal expenses.
2. **Pay Quarterly Estimated Taxes:** The IRS requires quarterly payments on April 15, June 16, September 15, and January 15 if you expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax.
3. **Log Every Business Expense:** Use [bro.tax's Write-off Library](/deductions) to ensure you never miss a legal deduction.
