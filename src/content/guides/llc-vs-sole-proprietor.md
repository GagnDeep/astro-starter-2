## The #1 LLC Tax Myth Debunked

> **Myth:** *"Forming an LLC saves me thousands of dollars on taxes!"*
> **Fact:** A single-member Limited Liability Company (LLC) is classified by the IRS as a **disregarded entity**. For federal tax purposes, a single-member LLC pays the **EXACT SAME tax rate** as a solo freelancer with no LLC.

Let's clear up the confusion so you don't waste $500 on state filing fees thinking it gives you secret tax write-offs.

---

## Sole Proprietorship vs Single-Member LLC Comparison

| Feature | Sole Proprietorship | Single-Member LLC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Status** | You and the business are legally the same entity. | Separate legal entity formed under state law. |
| **Liability Protection**| Personal assets (savings, car) at risk if sued. | Personal assets separated from business liabilities (if maintained properly). |
| **Federal IRS Tax Filing**| Form 1040 Schedule C | Form 1040 Schedule C *(Identical!)* |
| **Self-Employment Tax Rate**| **15.3%** on net profit | **15.3%** on net profit *(Identical!)* |
| **Write-off Eligibility**| 100% of valid business expenses | 100% of valid business expenses *(Identical!)* |
| **State Filing Fees** | $0 (No formation required) | $50–$800/year depending on state (e.g. CA $800 franchise tax) |

## When Should You Form an LLC?

Forming an LLC is a **legal protection decision**, not a tax decision.

You should form an LLC if:
1. You perform work that carries liability risk (e.g. medical advice, structural work, handling sensitive client user data).
2. You sign large commercial contracts with corporate clients who mandate an LLC or business entity.
3. You want a distinct business bank account and formal brand structure.

## What About the S-Corp Election?

If your net self-employment profit exceeds **$80,000 to $100,000 per year**, electing S-Corporation tax status (IRS Form 2553) can reduce self-employment taxes by splitting income between a reasonable W-2 salary and owner distribution draws.

However, S-Corps require payroll software fees ($500+/yr), annual corporate tax filings (Form 1120-S), and strict accounting. Below $80,000 in net profit, S-Corp compliance overhead costs usually outweigh tax savings.
