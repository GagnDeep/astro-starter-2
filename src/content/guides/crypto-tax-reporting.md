# Crypto Tax Reporting

Cryptocurrency transactions can complicate an otherwise simple tax return. Here is what you need to know to avoid paying excessive software fees.

## Overview
The IRS treats cryptocurrency as property. This means that selling crypto, trading one crypto for another, or using crypto to buy goods triggers a [Capital Gain](/glossary/capital-gain/) or loss.

### Filing Requirements
Every time you dispose of crypto, you must report it on Schedule D and Form 8949.
- If you simply *bought* crypto with USD and held it, you do not need to report it.
- If you earned crypto from mining or staking, it is reported as ordinary income.

### Free Filing with Crypto
Commercial tax software often forces you into their most expensive "Premium" tiers the moment you mention cryptocurrency. However, if your AGI is under $79,000, you can use [IRS Free File](/guides/irs-free-file/) to file Schedule D for absolutely free.
