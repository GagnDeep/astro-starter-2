The Cayman Islands operates a world-class Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) regime. The jurisdiction's removal from the FATF "Grey List" in late 2023 underscores the strict enforcement of these laws. This guide provides an overview of the core legislative framework.

## The Legislative Framework

The regime is built on three main pillars:
1.  **The Proceeds of Crime Act (POCA):** The primary legislation that defines money laundering offenses and establishes the Financial Reporting Authority (FRA).
2.  **The Anti-Money Laundering Regulations (AMLRs):** The detailed regulations that dictate how financial institutions must implement compliance programs.
3.  **CIMA Guidance Notes:** Practical guidance issued by the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority detailing how to apply the AMLRs using a risk-based approach.

## Who Must Comply?

The AMLRs apply to any person conducting "Relevant Financial Business" in or from the Cayman Islands. This captures almost the entire offshore financial sector:
*   Banks, Trust Companies, and Insurance Companies.
*   Mutual Funds and Private Funds.
*   Corporate Service Providers.
*   Real Estate Agents and High Value Dealers (e.g., precious metals dealers).
*   Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs).

## Core Requirements of the AMLRs

If an entity is in scope, it must establish and maintain the following procedures:

### 1. The Risk-Based Approach
Entities cannot apply a one-size-fits-all compliance program. They must conduct a formalized risk assessment of their clients, countries of operation, products, and delivery channels, and tailor their due diligence accordingly.

### 2. Customer Due Diligence (CDD / KYC)
Entities must identify and verify the identity of their customers, including understanding the ownership and control structure of corporate clients to identify the Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBOs) at a 10% threshold.

### 3. Named AML Officers
The entity must formally appoint individuals to act as the Anti-Money Laundering Compliance Officer (AMLCO), Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO), and Deputy MLRO.

### 4. Ongoing Monitoring
CDD is not a one-time event at onboarding. Entities must continuously monitor business relationships and scrutinize transactions to ensure they are consistent with the customer's known risk profile and source of funds.

### 5. Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR)
If an entity knows or suspects that funds are the proceeds of criminal conduct, they are legally obligated to file a SAR with the Cayman Islands Financial Reporting Authority (FRA). Crucially, it is a criminal offense to "tip off" the client that a SAR has been filed.

## The "Reliance" and "Delegation" Models

For investment funds without their own staff, complying with the AMLRs is achieved through outsourcing.

*   **Delegation:** The fund delegates the mechanical aspects of KYC collection to a service provider (like an administrator). The fund retains ultimate legal responsibility and must audit the provider.
*   **Reliance:** The fund relies on a regulated third party (e.g., an onshore bank or broker) who has already conducted CDD on the investor. The Cayman fund must ensure the third party operates in an "equivalent jurisdiction" with AML laws as strict as Cayman's.

## Enforcement and Fines

CIMA possesses administrative fining powers, meaning they can levy massive financial penalties without going through the criminal courts.
*   Fines for systemic AML failures frequently reach hundreds of thousands of dollars and have occasionally exceeded CI$1 million for major corporate service providers.
