The **Securities Investment Business Act (SIBA)** is the primary legislation governing individuals and entities that conduct securities investment business in or from the Cayman Islands. If you are an investment manager, broker, or financial advisor operating a Cayman entity, you must navigate SIBA.

## What is "Securities Investment Business"?

SIBA defines "securities" broadly, including shares, partnership interests, trust units, debt instruments, options, futures, and contracts for difference.

You are conducting securities investment business if, by way of business, you:
1.  **Deal in securities** (buying, selling, or acting as a broker).
2.  **Arrange deals** in securities.
3.  **Manage securities** (discretionary portfolio management, typically the role of a fund manager).
4.  **Advise on securities** (providing tailored investment advice).

## The Three Tiers of SIBA Regulation

If a Cayman entity conducts any of the above activities, it falls into one of three categories:

### 1. Fully Licensed
Entities providing services to the general retail public must apply for a full SIBA license from CIMA. This involves intense regulatory scrutiny, high capital adequacy requirements, and strict operational mandates. Very few offshore fund managers require a full license.

### 2. Excluded Persons (Non-Registrable)
Certain entities are entirely excluded from the registration requirements of SIBA. The most common example is an entity that acts as an investment manager *only* for a group of companies within its own corporate family (e.g., a proprietary trading firm or a single-family office).

### 3. Registered Persons (The "Exempt" Route)
This is the category that applies to almost all Cayman Islands investment managers and advisors to hedge funds and private equity funds.

Previously known as "Exempted Persons," the law was updated to require these entities to become **Registered Persons**. You qualify as a Registered Person if you provide securities investment business exclusively to:
*   Sophisticated persons (high-net-worth individuals).
*   High Net Worth persons (net worth over US$1M).
*   Regulated entities (like a CIMA-registered mutual fund or private fund).

## Requirements for Registered Persons

To become a Registered Person under SIBA, an entity must apply to CIMA via the REEFS portal. The obligations are significant:

1.  **Directors:** The entity must have a minimum of two directors (or one corporate director). Crucially, these directors must be registered or licensed under the Directors Registration and Licensing Act (DRLA).
2.  **AML/CFT:** The entity is subject to the full weight of the Cayman Islands Anti-Money Laundering Regulations. It must appoint an AMLCO, MLRO, and DMLRO, and maintain a written AML manual.
3.  **Economic Substance:** A SIBA Registered Person acting as a discretionary fund manager is conducting the relevant activity of "Fund Management Business." It must therefore satisfy the Economic Substance test, which requires demonstrating adequate physical presence, employees, and operating expenditure in the Cayman Islands.
4.  **Annual Fees:** A Registered Person must pay an annual fee of CI$5,000 to CIMA and file an annual declaration confirming it continues to meet the criteria.

## The "Non-Cayman Manager" Scenario

It is vital to understand that SIBA applies to entities incorporated in the Cayman Islands, or foreign entities physically operating in the Cayman Islands.

Most Cayman Islands investment funds are managed by an investment manager incorporated and physically located in New York, London, or Hong Kong. In this scenario, the onshore investment manager does **not** need to register under SIBA, as it is not a Cayman entity and is not operating *from* the Cayman Islands.

## Penalties

Conducting securities investment business in or from the Cayman Islands without the appropriate SIBA license or registration is a criminal offense, carrying severe financial penalties and potential imprisonment for directors.
