The Cayman Islands places strict obligations on individuals serving as directors for regulated entities. The cornerstone of this regulatory framework is the **Directors Registration and Licensing Act (DRLA)**, administered by the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA).

## The Directors Registration and Licensing Act (DRLA)

If you act as a director of a "Covered Entity," you must be registered or licensed under the DRLA before you begin your duties.

### What is a Covered Entity?
*   A mutual fund regulated under the Mutual Funds Act.
*   A private fund regulated under the Private Funds Act.
*   Certain entities registered under the Securities Investment Business Act (SIBA).

*Note: If you are a director of an ordinary Exempted Company that is NOT a regulated fund or SIBA entity, the DRLA does not apply to you.*

### Registration vs. Licensing

1.  **Registered Director:** An individual acting as a director for fewer than 20 covered entities. Registration is an online process via the CIMA Director Gateway.
2.  **Professional Director:** An individual acting as a director for 20 or more covered entities. This requires a full *license* (unless you work for a licensed fund administrator). The licensing process involves deeper scrutiny, fit-and-proper tests, and higher fees.
3.  **Corporate Director:** Any corporate entity acting as a director must be licensed, regardless of the number of directorships held.

### The Application Process and Costs

*   **Timeline:** Registration usually takes 48 hours once submitted via the Gateway. Licensing can take 4-6 weeks.
*   **Fees (2024):**
    *   Application Fee for Registration: CI$700 (~US$853)
    *   Annual Renewal Fee (due Jan 15): CI$700
    *   Professional Director License: CI$3,000 application + CI$3,000 annual.

## Fiduciary Duties under Cayman Law

Cayman Islands corporate law relies heavily on English common law precedents. Directors owe fiduciary duties directly to the company, not to the shareholders (unless specifically agreed otherwise).

1.  **Duty to act in good faith in the best interests of the company.**
2.  **Duty to exercise powers for a proper purpose.**
3.  **Duty to avoid conflicts of interest.**
4.  **Duty not to make secret profits from the position.**
5.  **Duty of skill, care, and diligence.**

**The Weavering Case:** The most famous Cayman case regarding director duties is *Weavering Macro Fixed Income Fund*. The court ruled that independent directors must actually supervise the service providers (investment manager, administrator). They cannot simply act as "rubber stamps." This case fundamentally changed the standard of care expected of independent directors in Cayman.

## AML and Compliance Responsibilities

Under the Anti-Money Laundering Regulations, the ultimate responsibility for an entity's AML compliance rests with the Board of Directors.

The Board must:
*   Appoint qualified individuals as AMLCO, MLRO, and DMLRO.
*   Review AML reports from the AMLCO at least annually (usually quarterly in practice).
*   Ensure the entity is conducting regular risk assessments.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to live in the Cayman Islands to be a director?**
No. There is no residency requirement for directors of standard Exempted Companies or regulated funds. However, Economic Substance rules may dictate that board meetings occur physically in the Cayman Islands.

**Can I resign to avoid paying the annual DRLA fee?**
To avoid the January 15th renewal fee, you must resign from all covered entities, file the deregistration application, and pay a surrender fee (CI$600) before December 31st of the preceding year. If you deregister on January 2nd, you owe the full year's fee.

**What happens if I forget to pay my annual DRLA fee?**
CIMA applies automatic penalties for late payment. A surcharge of one-twelfth of the fee applies for every month it is late. Continued failure to pay can result in your registration being cancelled, preventing you from acting for any Cayman fund.
