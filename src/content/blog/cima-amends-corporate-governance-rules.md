The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) has finalized its highly anticipated updates to the regulatory framework governing corporate governance for regulated entities, including Mutual Funds, Private Funds, and SIBA Registered Persons.

The new **Rule and Statement of Guidance on Corporate Governance** replaces the previous 2016 framework and imposes stricter, more explicit obligations on governing bodies (Boards of Directors, General Partners).

## The Key Changes

### 1. Documented Conflicts of Interest
The new rule explicitly requires that a regulated entity's governing body establish a documented Conflicts of Interest policy. It is no longer sufficient to simply declare conflicts at a board meeting; the entity must have a written policy detailing how conflicts are identified, managed, and mitigated. For investment funds where the investment manager and the board share principals, this requires careful drafting.

### 2. Outsourcing Oversight
Given that the vast majority of Cayman funds outsource their core functions (investment management, administration, AML compliance), CIMA has cracked down on the "rubber stamp" board. Governing bodies must now formally review the performance of outsourced service providers at least annually.

**What this means:** You can outsource the *function*, but you cannot outsource the *responsibility*. Directors must document their reviews of administrator SLA metrics and compliance reports.

### 3. Board Composition and Expertise
The rule requires that the governing body, as a whole, possesses the necessary skills, knowledge, and experience to fulfill its duties. CIMA has signaled it will scrutinize boards that lack diversity of expertise or where a single individual holds too much concentrated power without adequate checks and balances.

### 4. Meeting Frequency and Minutes
The previous guidance suggested meeting at least twice a year. The new rule maintains this frequency for mutual funds but explicitly extends it to private funds. Furthermore, the rule prescribes specific details that *must* be included in the board minutes, including declarations of conflicts, review of service providers, and review of AML/CFT reports.

## Impact on Private Funds

The most significant shift is the formal extension of these stringent corporate governance requirements to Private Funds (closed-ended private equity and venture capital funds), which only came under CIMA regulation in 2020.

Historically, PE fund governance was largely dictated by the Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA) rather than statutory rules. Now, the General Partner of a Private Fund must adhere to the same documented corporate governance standards as the board of a liquid hedge fund.

## Action Steps for Fund Managers

1.  **Review Governance Manuals:** Ensure you have a formal, written corporate governance framework that addresses conflicts of interest and outsourcing oversight.
2.  **Agenda Updates:** Ensure your bi-annual board meeting agendas specifically include a review of the AMLCO's report and a review of the administrator/investment manager's performance.
3.  **Document Everything:** In a CIMA inspection, if it isn't in the board minutes, it didn't happen.
