For expatriate professionals relocating to the Cayman Islands with children, securing housing is only the second hardest task. The primary challenge—and often the deciding factor on whether an executive accepts a job offer—is securing a spot in a private school.

As the financial services sector expands and post-pandemic migration continues, Cayman's premier private schools are operating at full capacity, with waitlists stretching into the hundreds.

## The Public vs. Private Divide

As outlined in our [Education Guide](/living/schools-education/), expatriate children (those whose parents are on work permits) are generally not permitted to attend government public schools. They must attend fee-paying private schools.

The top-tier schools—Cayman International School (CIS), Cayman Prep and High School, and St. Ignatius—are the primary targets for corporate expats.

## The State of the Waitlists

The situation is most acute in the primary years (Years 1 through 6).
*   **CIS:** Despite a massive recent campus expansion, CIS has waitlists across almost all primary grades.
*   **Cayman Prep:** Historically oversubscribed, Prep prioritizes siblings of existing students and members of the affiliated church, making it exceptionally difficult for new arrivals without local connections to secure a spot.

## Strategies for New Arrivals

Human Resources departments at major law firms and accounting firms advise incoming staff to tackle the school issue before their work permit is even submitted.

1.  **Apply Early and Broadly:** Do not wait until you arrive on the island. Submit applications (and pay the non-refundable application fees) to at least three different schools the moment you sign your employment contract.
2.  **Corporate Debentures:** Some of the largest firms on the island (the "Big Four" accounting firms, top-tier offshore law firms) hold corporate debentures with schools like CIS. These debentures guarantee a certain number of spots for the children of their employees. If you are negotiating a senior package, ask your prospective employer if they hold debenture seats.
3.  **Look Beyond the 'Big Three':** The intense focus on CIS, Prep, and St. Ignatius often blinds expats to alternative options. Schools like Triple C (American curriculum) and Hope Academy are excellent alternatives that occasionally have more flexible enrollment capacity.

## The WORC Factor

The Department of Workforce Opportunities & Residency Cayman (WORC) is acutely aware of the capacity issue. When processing a dependent work permit application, WORC will scrutinize the parent's salary to ensure they can afford private education. Increasingly, WORC is asking for proof of school enrollment or waitlist status before approving the dependent permit, creating a stressful chicken-and-egg scenario for relocating families.

Families must budget aggressively; with high school fees exceeding CI$20,000 per year per child, dual-income households are becoming increasingly necessary to maintain a comfortable expat lifestyle.
