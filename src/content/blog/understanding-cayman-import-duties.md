For new residents, the sticker shock of local retail prices often drives them immediately to US e-commerce sites. However, without understanding how Cayman Islands Customs calculates duty, that "cheap" Amazon purchase can quickly become an expensive mistake.

## The CIF Calculation

The most common mistake expats make is assuming duty is charged only on the purchase price of the item. It is not.

Cayman Customs charges duty on the **CIF value**:
*   **C = Cost:** The price you paid for the item.
*   **I = Insurance:** The cost of any shipping insurance.
*   **F = Freight:** The cost of shipping the item from the supplier to the Cayman Islands.

**Example:** You buy a $500 TV. You pay $50 to ship it to your Miami forwarder, and the forwarder charges $100 to ship it to Cayman.
*   Your CIF is $650.
*   The standard 22% duty is calculated on $650 (resulting in $143 duty), *not* on the $500 purchase price.
*   Your total landed cost is $500 + $50 + $100 + $143 = **$793**.

## Hidden Fees

In addition to the 22% customs duty, your freight forwarder will charge handling fees, customs clearance fees, and sometimes local delivery fees. Furthermore, the Customs department charges a small "Customs Processing Fee" per declaration.

## When to Buy Local vs. Import

### Import:
*   **Electronics:** Laptops, cameras, and specialist electronics are often cheaper to import, particularly because items like computers are often duty-free (though you still pay freight).
*   **Clothing:** Due to a lack of mid-tier retail options on the island, clothing is almost always imported via forwarders.

### Buy Local:
*   **Heavy/Bulky Items:** Furniture, large appliances, and TVs. The "Freight" portion of the CIF calculation for ocean freight is based on cubic volume. Shipping a cheap, massive couch from IKEA will result in a freight bill that vastly exceeds the cost of the couch, driving the CIF duty through the roof. It is often cheaper (and less stressful regarding damages) to buy from local retailers like Marksons or A.L. Thompson's.
