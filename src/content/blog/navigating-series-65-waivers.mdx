## Do you really need to sit for the Series 65 exam?

When transitioning to an independent RIA, one of the first questions managing partners ask is whether they need to take the Uniform Investment Adviser Law Examination (Series 65). This exam tests knowledge of the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 and state laws, and it is a requirement for providing investment advice for a fee unless you hold a qualifying professional designation.

The good news is that most state regulators provide immediate waivers if you hold specific, active designations such as the CFP, CFA, CIC, ChFC, or PFS.

However, the application of these waivers is not entirely uniform across all 50 states, and a lapsed Series 7 coupled with a Series 66 can complicate your transition timeline.

### What are the exact requirements for a Series 65 waiver?

To qualify for a waiver, you must hold one of the recognized professional designations (CFP, CFA, CIC, ChFC, or PFS) in good standing. This means your dues must be paid, your continuing education must be up to date, and you cannot have any disciplinary actions that have suspended or revoked your credential.

Below is an original comparison table compiled by the Institute of RIA, tracking the acceptance of the top three designations across key financial hub states.

| State | Accepts CFP Waiver | Accepts CFA Waiver | Accepts ChFC Waiver | Special Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **New York** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Requires Form U4 submission prior to granting the waiver. |
| **California** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Requires the designation to have been active for at least 12 months. |
| **Texas** | Yes | Yes | Yes | None. Standard U4 processing. |

_Note: This table reflects state regulatory stances as of August 2024. Always verify directly with your state securities regulator before relying on a waiver._

## How does a lapsed Series 7 impact your transition to an RIA?

If you are breaking away from a broker-dealer, you likely hold a Series 7 (General Securities Representative) and a Series 66 (Uniform Combined State Law Examination). While at the broker-dealer, this combination satisfies the requirement to act as an Investment Adviser Representative.

However, once you leave the broker-dealer, your Series 7 will typically expire after a two-year grace period. Once the Series 7 lapses, the Series 66 alone is insufficient to maintain your IAR registration in many states. If you do not obtain a qualifying professional designation before the Series 7 expires, you may be required to sit for the Series 65 to continue operating your [RIA firm](/formation/).

### What is the process for submitting a waiver request through the CRD?

The process for claiming your waiver is handled entirely through the Central Registration Depository (CRD) system via the filing of your Form U4.

When completing the U4, you will select the specific state(s) where you are seeking IAR registration. In the 'Professional Designations' section of the form, you must accurately report your active credential (e.g., your CFP certification number). The system automatically flags this to the relevant state examiners.

Most states process these waiver requests seamlessly as part of your overall registration approval, but it can occasionally add a few days to your timeline if a state requests additional proof of good standing. ## Preparing for the worst-case scenario: Taking the Series 65

If you do not hold a qualifying designation and your prior licenses have lapsed, you must pass the Series 65. The exam consists of 130 scored questions, and you must achieve a score of at least 72% (94 correct answers) to pass.

We strongly recommend dedicating at least 60 to 80 hours of focused study time, utilizing a reputable third-party test prep provider. Failing the exam requires a 30-day waiting period before a retake, which can severely disrupt your [compliance](/compliance/) timeline and your firm's launch date.
