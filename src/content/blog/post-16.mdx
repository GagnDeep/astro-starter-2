You receive an email from your doctor or a government agency with an attached PDF form. You open it, click the first line to type your name, and nothing happens. It's a "flat" PDF.

## The Difference Between Flat and Interactive Forms

An interactive form (known technically as an [AcroForm](/reference/topic-18)) has embedded logic that defines specific areas as text boxes, checkboxes, or dropdowns. A flat PDF is basically a digital photograph of a piece of paper.

## How to Fill It Out

You don't need to buy a printer just to fill out a form.

1. Go to our [Add Text Tool](/tools/add-text-to-pdf).
2. Upload the flat PDF.
3. Select the Text icon.
4. Click near the line you want to fill and start typing.
5. Drag the text box into perfect alignment.

### Adding Checkmarks and Signatures

If the form has checkboxes, you can use the Text tool to type an "X", or use the Freehand Drawing tool to draw a checkmark.

For signatures, select the Freehand tool, adjust the line thickness, and sign with your mouse or trackpad. When you save the document, all your additions are merged into the file's [Annots array](/reference/topic-1).

For a more detailed walkthrough, see our [Non-Interactive Form Guide](/guides/guide-3).
