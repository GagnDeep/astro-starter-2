# Preparing for the 2027 Battery Passport Deadline

By February 18, 2027, the battery industry will undergo a massive shift. The EU Battery Regulation mandates that every EV and LMT battery sold in the internal market must be accompanied by a Digital Product Passport (DPP). This is not merely a labeling exercise; it is a fundamental re-engineering of how data moves through the supply chain.

This post covers the critical aspects of preparing for this deadline, moving beyond the legal text to practical implementation strategies.

## The Problem: Data Fragmentation

Most operators underestimate the complexity of data gathering. The primary source (EU Regulation 2023/1542) mandates over 90 specific data points. Currently, this data is heavily fragmented:

- **Chemistry and bill of materials:** Locked in PLM (Product Lifecycle Management) systems.
- **Manufacturing dates and batches:** Tracked in ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) systems.
- **Scope 3 emissions and origin:** Buried in supplier PDFs and procurement emails.
- **State of Health:** Managed dynamically by the BMS (Battery Management System).

The Battery Passport requires synthesizing these disparate data streams into a single, interoperable, role-based access system.

## The 2027 Roadmap

To avoid missing the deadline, economic operators should follow a phased approach.

### Phase 1: Gap Analysis (2024)

1. **Map the Data Model:** Download the draft data dictionary (e.g., from the Battery Pass Consortium) and map it against your internal systems.
2. **Identify Blind Spots:** You will likely discover that you have zero visibility into your Tier-3 suppliers' carbon emissions or human rights due diligence.
3. **Engage Legal:** Determine definitively if you are the "economic operator placing the battery on the market."

### Phase 2: Pilot and Procurement (2025)

2025 is critical because the **Carbon Footprint Declaration** becomes mandatory for EV batteries on February 18, 2025.

1. **Deploy a Traceability Tool:** You cannot manage this in Excel. Evaluate vendors like [Circulor](/vendors/circulor/) or others listed in our [Vendor Directory](/vendors/).
2. **Run a Single-Line Pilot:** Select one battery model and attempt to generate a fully compliant passport for it. This will stress-test your supplier relationships.
3. **Update Supplier Contracts:** Mandate the provision of primary carbon and recycled content data in all new procurement contracts.

### Phase 3: Scaling and Integration (2026)

1. **Automate Data Flows:** Integrate your PLM and ERP systems with your chosen passport platform using APIs.
2. **BMS Integration:** Ensure your battery firmware can securely transmit State of Health (SoH) metrics to the passport system over the air.
3. **Pre-Audit:** Engage a Notified Body to conduct a mock audit of your passport generation process.

## Common Mistakes

- **Ignoring the 2025 deadlines:** Many operators focus entirely on the 2027 passport deadline and miss the 2025 carbon footprint requirement, which is a prerequisite.
- **Assuming existing ERP systems are sufficient:** Standard ERPs lack the cryptographic security and role-based public access required for a decentralized passport.
- **Failing to secure supplier data early:** Tier-2 and Tier-3 suppliers in Asia may not be prepared to provide primary data. If they fail, you bear the compliance risk.

## Edge Cases

**What happens if a supplier outside the EU refuses to provide carbon data?**
You must prove due diligence. If primary data is truly unavailable, the JRC methodology permits the use of secondary (industry average) data, but applies a penalty factor. This artificially inflates your reported carbon footprint, which could eventually prevent your battery from being sold when maximum carbon thresholds are enforced.

## Conclusion

The 2027 deadline seems distant, but the structural changes required in your supply chain data architecture take years to implement. Start your gap analysis today.
