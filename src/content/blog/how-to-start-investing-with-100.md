There is a pervasive myth that you need thousands of dollars to start investing. Ten years ago, that might have been true, due to trading commissions and high share prices. Today, it is entirely false.

Thanks to the advent of **fractional shares** and zero-commission trading, you can start investing with as little as $1. Here is exactly how to start with your first $100.

### Step 1: Open a Brokerage Account

You need a platform to buy and sell investments. For a beginner starting with $100, your primary concern should be zero fees and access to fractional shares.

Fidelity and Charles Schwab are excellent, established choices. Both offer zero-commission trading on US stocks and ETFs, and both offer fractional shares (meaning if a stock costs $400, you can just buy $10 worth of it).

*Action:* Go to Fidelity.com or Schwab.com and open a standard brokerage account. It takes about 10 minutes.

### Step 2: Fund the Account

Link your checking account to your new brokerage account. Transfer your $100. It may take 1-3 business days for the funds to fully settle and become available to invest.

*Important Note:* Transferring money to your brokerage account **does not mean it is invested**. It just means it is sitting as cash inside your brokerage account. You still have to buy something.

### Step 3: Choose a Broad Market Index Fund

With $100, you shouldn't try to build a complex portfolio. You should buy a single fund that gives you maximum diversification.

An S&P 500 ETF (like VOO or IVV) or a Total Stock Market ETF (like VTI or ITOT) is perfect.
* **VOO (Vanguard S&P 500 ETF):** Holds the 500 largest US companies.
* **VTI (Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF):** Holds almost every publicly traded US company (over 3,500 stocks).

Either one is an excellent starting point.

### Step 4: Buy Fractional Shares

Let's say VOO is currently trading at $450 per share. You only have $100.

1. Log into your brokerage.
2. Search for the ticker symbol "VOO".
3. Click "Buy".
4. Select "Dollars" instead of "Shares" (this is how you access fractional shares).
5. Enter $100.
6. Review the order and submit.

Congratulations. You now own a fraction of a share of VOO, which means you own a tiny piece of Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and 497 other major companies.

### Step 5: Automate and Ignore

The final step is the most important. Investing $100 once won't make you rich. Investing $100 *every month* for 30 years will.

Set up an automatic transfer from your checking account to your brokerage account, and see if your broker supports automatic investments (buying that same ETF automatically every month). Once it's set up, ignore the market news and let the power of compound interest take over.
