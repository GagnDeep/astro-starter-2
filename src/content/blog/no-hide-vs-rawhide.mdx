## The Rawhide Problem

For decades, rawhide was the default chew for dogs. It’s cheap, lasts forever, and keeps dogs busy. But over the last few years, pet owners have started asking questions about what rawhide actually is.

Rawhide is not meat. It’s a byproduct of the leather industry. The inner layer of cow or horse hide is cleaned, often bleached with chemicals like hydrogen peroxide, and glued into shapes using adhesives.

### Why Rawhide is Risky
1. **Choking Hazard:** As dogs chew rawhide, it gets soggy and stringy. Dogs can swallow large, gummy chunks that get lodged in their throat.
2. **Blockages:** Rawhide doesn't break down easily in a dog's stomach. A swallowed chunk can expand in the digestive tract, causing life-threatening blockages that require surgery.
3. **Chemicals:** Depending on where the rawhide is manufactured, it may contain traces of heavy metals, bleach, or formaldehyde used in the tanning process.

## Enter "No Hide"

As the dangers of rawhide became known, the pet industry responded with "No Hide" chews. The name implies it’s a safer, hide-free alternative.

### What is a No Hide Chew?
A No Hide chew is typically made of a few simple ingredients: rice flour, eggs, olive oil, and a flavor protein (like beef, chicken, or salmon), gelatinized and rolled to look exactly like rawhide.

### The No Hide Advantage
The primary benefit of No Hide is **digestibility**. Because it is made of food ingredients rather than leather, a dog’s stomach acids can break it down if a piece is swallowed whole.

### The Tradeoffs of No Hide
1. **Durability:** No Hide chews do not last as long as rawhide. For a power chewer, a No Hide might last 10 minutes, compared to a rawhide that could last days.
2. **Cost:** Because they use real food ingredients, No Hide chews are significantly more expensive.
3. **Calories:** No Hide chews are food, which means they have calories. A medium No Hide chew can contain over 150 calories, which must be factored into your dog's treat allowance.

## Comparison Table

| Feature | Rawhide | No Hide |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Material** | Leather byproduct | Rice flour, gelatin, meat |
| **Digestibility** | Very Low | High |
| **Blockage Risk** | High | Low |
| **Durability** | High | Low-Medium |
| **Cost** | Low | High |

## Common Mistakes
- **Assuming No Hide means No Choking Risk:** Any chew can be a choking hazard if your dog tries to swallow the last piece whole. Always supervise chewing and take away small nubs.
- **Ignoring Calories:** No Hide chews are dense in calories. Don't feed them daily if your dog is prone to weight gain.

## What to Do Next
If you've been feeding rawhide, throw it out. It's simply not worth the risk of a blockage.

If you are looking for alternatives:
- **For heavy chewers:** Consider a durable rubber toy (like a Kong) stuffed with wet food and frozen.
- **For natural chews:** Look into Bully Sticks, Beef Trachea, or single-ingredient jerky.
