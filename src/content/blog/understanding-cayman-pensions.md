Unlike many offshore jurisdictions, the Cayman Islands has a robust, mandatory National Pensions Law designed to ensure residents save for retirement. For expats moving to the island, understanding how this impacts take-home pay is crucial.

## The 5% + 5% Rule

The law requires that 10% of an employee's earnings be contributed to a registered pension plan.
*   **Employer Contribution:** The employer must pay at least 5%.
*   **Employee Contribution:** The employee pays a maximum of 5% (deducted directly from their salary).

*Note: Some generous employers will cover the entire 10% contribution, meaning there is zero deduction from the employee's gross pay.*

## Who is Exempt?

Not everyone has to pay a pension immediately.
1.  **Expats on a Work Permit:** Do not have to contribute for their first 9 months of employment in the Cayman Islands. After 9 months, contributions become mandatory.
2.  **Domestic Helpers:** Are exempt from the mandatory pension law.

## What Happens When You Leave?

This is the most common question among expats. If you leave the Cayman Islands permanently, you cannot immediately cash out your pension.
*   **The 2-Year Rule:** Under the current law, you must wait until your employment has been terminated and you have been absent from the Cayman Islands for at least two years before you can apply for a lump-sum refund of your pension balance.
*   **Refund Limits:** You can generally withdraw your accumulated contributions and investment returns, subject to the specific rules of your pension provider.
