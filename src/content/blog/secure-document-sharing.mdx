Email was invented in the 1970s. It was designed to send plain text between researchers. It was *not* designed to securely transmit confidential business contracts, [NDAs](/templates/nda), or tax documents like [W-9s](/templates/w9).

Yet, every day, millions of small businesses attach highly sensitive PDFs to emails and hit send, hoping for the best.

In this post, we'll explain why emailing unsecured attachments is dangerous, and how to handle document sharing correctly.

## The Problem with Email Attachments

1. **Interception:** Standard email is often sent in plain text. If intercepted, anyone can read the attachment.
2. **Permanent Storage:** Once you email a PDF, it lives in the recipient's inbox (and their email provider's servers) forever. You lose all control over it.
3. **No Integrity:** If you email a contract for someone to sign, they can easily edit the text of the PDF before signing it, and you might not notice.

## The Solution: Link-Based Sharing with Encryption

Instead of sending the document *itself*, you should send a *secure link* to the document hosted on an encrypted platform.

This is exactly how [usignpdf](/) operates.

### How usignpdf protects your documents:

1. **Encryption at Rest:** When you upload a document to usignpdf, it is immediately encrypted using AES-256 (the same standard used by the military).
2. **Encryption in Transit:** When the recipient clicks the link, the document is transmitted to their browser over TLS 1.3. (Read more on our [Encryption page](/security/encryption)).
3. **Document Integrity:** Because the document is hosted on our servers, the recipient cannot edit the underlying text. They can only place their signature where allowed.
4. **Tracking:** You get a full [Audit Trail](/security/audit-trails) showing exactly when the document was opened and signed (see [How to track document opens](/blog/how-to-track-document-opens)).

## What about Enterprise Tools?

Tools like [DocuSign](/compare/docusign) and [HelloSign](/compare/hellosign) also use secure link sharing. However, they often force the recipient to create an account, verify their email, and jump through hoops before they can even read the document.

For [freelancers](/use-cases/freelancers) and small agencies, this creates massive friction during client onboarding.

usignpdf offers the same AES-256 security and cryptographic hashing as the enterprise tools, but removes the login barrier for the signer.

## Best Practices for Sharing Sensitive Docs

- **Never email passwords.** Use a tool like 1Password.
- **Never email unencrypted tax forms.** A W-9 contains a Social Security Number or EIN.
- **Always use a dedicated e-signature platform** to capture intent and maintain compliance with laws like the [ESIGN Act](/legality/us-esign-act) and [GDPR](/compliance/gdpr).
