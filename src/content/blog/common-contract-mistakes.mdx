Contracts are the guardrails of business. When things go well, they sit in a digital drawer. When things go poorly, they are the only thing standing between you and significant financial loss.

Despite their importance, small businesses and [freelancers](/use-cases/freelancers) consistently make basic errors in their contracts.

Here are the top five contract mistakes we see, and exactly how to fix them.

## Mistake 1: Relying on Verbal Agreements

"We had a great chat, they agreed to the price on the phone, so I started work."

This is the most common and most dangerous mistake. Verbal contracts are notoriously difficult to enforce. If a dispute arises, it becomes a "he said, she said" scenario, and courts require evidence.

**The Fix:** Put absolutely everything in writing. Even if you discuss scope changes on a call, follow up with an email stating: "As discussed, we are adding X to the project for $Y." Then, get an official sign-off using a tool like [usignpdf](/).

## Mistake 2: Missing Intellectual Property Assignments

If you hire an [agency](/use-cases/agencies) or a contractor to design a logo or write code, you assume you own it once you pay them.

Under US law, this is incorrect. The creator owns the copyright by default.

**The Fix:** Your [independent contractor agreement](/blog/independent-contractor-agreement-guide) must contain a specific clause assigning all Intellectual Property (IP) rights to you upon payment.

## Mistake 3: Unclear Payment Terms

"Payment is $5,000" is not a payment term.

When is it due? What happens if it's late? Do they pay upfront?

**The Fix:** Break payment terms down clearly:
- 50% deposit required before work commences (non-refundable).
- 50% due upon delivery of final assets.
- Invoices are Net-15 (due 15 days after issuance).
- Late payments accrue a 1.5% interest fee per month.

## Mistake 4: Using the Wrong E-Signature Method

Many people think pasting a JPEG of their signature into a Word document makes it a legally binding contract. It does not. It completely lacks [non-repudiation](/glossary#non-repudiation).

Furthermore, using iOS markup or basic PDF editors provides no proof of *who* actually signed it.

**The Fix:** You must use an [electronic signature](/glossary#electronic-signature) platform that tracks the signing event. [usignpdf](/) generates a secure [Audit Trail](/security/audit-trails) logging the IP address, timestamp, and a [cryptographic hash](/glossary#cryptographic-hash) of the document, ensuring it complies with the [ESIGN Act](/legality/us-esign-act).

## Mistake 5: No "Kill Fee" or Termination Clause

Projects get cancelled. Budgets get frozen. If a client kills a project when you are 80% done, do you get paid?

**The Fix:** Include a termination clause. It should state that either party can terminate the agreement with written notice, and that the client is responsible for paying for all work completed up to the date of termination, at a specified hourly rate.
