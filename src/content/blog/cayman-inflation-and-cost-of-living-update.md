The Cayman Islands relies on importing virtually all its consumable goods, primarily from the United States. Consequently, the jurisdiction imports US inflation, which is then amplified by local import duties and freight costs. As we pass the midpoint of 2024, the cost of living remains the most pressing issue for the resident expat community.

## The Grocery Squeeze

Food prices remain stubbornly high. While the aggressive inflation spikes seen in 2022 and 2023 have cooled globally, prices in Cayman have plateaued at their new highs, not retreated.
*   Because the 22% import duty is applied to the CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) value, any increase in global shipping costs directly inflates the final retail price on the supermarket shelf. A standard basket of groceries that cost CI$150 in 2021 routinely costs over CI$220 today.

## The Housing Crunch

The real driver of the cost-of-living crisis is housing. The influx of new expatriate workers following the pandemic severely depleted the rental inventory.
*   **Rental Increases:** Landlords have capitalized on the shortage. It is common to see 1-bedroom apartments in the Seven Mile Beach corridor, which rented for CI$2,500/month three years ago, now listed at CI$4,000/month.
*   **The Utilities Multiplier:** As housing gets more expensive, so do the associated costs. Electricity (provided by CUC) remains a massive burden. Despite a slight drop in global fuel prices, the base rates and regulatory fees keep average monthly bills for a small apartment hovering between CI$300 and CI$500 during the hot summer months.

## Salary Pressures and the 'Expat Churn'

This inflationary environment is creating friction in the labor market.
*   Mid-level professionals (teachers, nurses, junior accountants) are finding it increasingly difficult to maintain a comfortable standard of living. This is leading to "expat churn"—where professionals leave the island after only a year or two because their savings capacity is entirely eroded by rent and groceries.
*   Employers in the financial services sector are being forced to dramatically increase their housing allowances and relocation packages to successfully recruit talent from London or Toronto.
