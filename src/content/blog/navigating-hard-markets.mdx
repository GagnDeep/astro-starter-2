## How Do Underwriters Survive a Hard Property Market?

A hard market in commercial property is characterized by shrinking capacity, tightening terms, and substantial rate increases. For the desk underwriter, this environment demands a return to fundamental risk selection principles.

When reinsurance treaty costs rise and management mandates immediate profitability, the margin for error disappears. Underwriters must rigorously enforce insurance-to-value (ITV) requirements, ensuring that replacement cost estimates are accurate in an inflationary environment.

Additionally, the strategic deployment of capacity becomes paramount. Instead of deploying maximum lines on marginal risks, underwriters must syndicate capacity, offering smaller slivers of coverage on a quota-share basis. This requires a strong understanding of layered program structures and the ability to negotiate attachment points effectively.

Finally, communication with distribution partners is critical. In a hard market, brokers face immense pressure from insureds facing premium shock. Underwriters who can clearly articulate the technical rationale behind their pricing and capacity decisions—rather than simply blaming "the market"—will build long-term, profitable relationships that endure once the market softens.
