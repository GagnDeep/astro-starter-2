A "robo-advisor" is an automated investment platform. You answer a few questions about your age, income, and risk tolerance, and an algorithm automatically builds and manages a diversified portfolio of index funds for you.

The two pioneers and current market leaders in this space are **Betterment** and **Wealthfront**.

They are brilliant pieces of technology. But are they worth the fees they charge?

### The Cost of a Robo-Advisor

Both Betterment and Wealthfront charge a **0.25% annual management fee**.

This fee is *in addition* to the expense ratios of the underlying ETFs they buy for you (which usually average around 0.05% to 0.10%).

So, if you use a robo-advisor, you are paying roughly 0.35% per year total. Compared to a human financial advisor (who typically charges 1.00% to 1.50%), a robo-advisor is a massive bargain.

But compared to doing it yourself at Fidelity or Vanguard (where you might pay 0.03%), a robo-advisor is expensive.

### What You Get for 0.25%

If you use Betterment or Wealthfront, the algorithm handles several things automatically:

1.  **Asset Allocation:** It decides exactly what percentage of your money should be in US Stocks, International Stocks, and Bonds based on your age.
2.  **Automatic Rebalancing:** If US stocks have a great year and suddenly make up 90% of your portfolio instead of the intended 80%, the algorithm automatically sells some US stocks and buys more bonds to return you to your target risk level.
3.  **Tax-Loss Harvesting:** (For taxable accounts only). If one of your funds drops in value, the algorithm will automatically sell it to capture the tax deduction, and immediately buy a very similar fund so you stay invested.

### Betterment vs Wealthfront

If you decide to use one, which is better?

*   **Wealthfront** is better for pure automation and high-yield cash. They offer a fantastic high-yield checking/savings account product alongside their investing platform, and their tax-loss harvesting algorithm is slightly more sophisticated.
*   **Betterment** is better for goal-setting. Their UI is heavily focused on specific goals (e.g., "Buy a House in 5 Years," "Retire at 65") and adjusts the risk of each bucket independently. They also offer access to human advisors if you upgrade to their premium tier (0.40% fee).

### The "Do It Yourself" Alternative

You can replicate 90% of what a robo-advisor does for free by simply buying a **Target Date Index Fund** at Vanguard, Fidelity, or Schwab.

A Target Date Fund automatically holds a mix of US stocks, international stocks, and bonds. It automatically rebalances. And it automatically becomes more conservative as you approach your retirement year.

If you buy a Vanguard Target Retirement Fund, you get all that automation for an expense ratio of 0.08%, with zero management fees.

### The Verdict

**Robo-advisors are worth the 0.25% fee IF they prevent you from making behavioral mistakes.**

If the beautiful UI of Betterment keeps you investing $500 a month, while the clunky UI of Vanguard makes you give up, then Betterment is absolutely worth the 0.25%.

However, if you are willing to spend 30 minutes learning how to buy a Target Date Index Fund or a basic 3-Fund Portfolio at a traditional brokerage, you can save yourself that 0.25% fee and keep more of your own money compounding over the next three decades.
