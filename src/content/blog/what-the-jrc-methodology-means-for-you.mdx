# What the JRC Methodology Means for You

The European Commission doesn't just mandate that you calculate a carbon footprint; they mandate *exactly how* you must calculate it. The rulebook is written by the Joint Research Centre (JRC).

If you attempt to use a generic Lifecycle Assessment (LCA) tool without strict adherence to the JRC's battery-specific rules (the PEFCR - Product Environmental Footprint Category Rules), your declaration will fail audit.

## The Three Pillars of the JRC Rules

### 1. The Functional Unit
Everything must be normalized to **1 kWh of total energy provided over the service life**. This requires knowing not just your manufacturing emissions, but the anticipated cycle life of the specific battery model you are building. A battery built with a highly carbon-intensive process might actually score well if its cycle life is double that of a competitor.

### 2. The Primary Data Mandate
The JRC actively combats "greenwashing via spreadsheet." You cannot look up the industry average carbon cost of producing NMC cathode powder and paste it into your report.

You must obtain **primary data** (actual meter readings from the specific factory floor) for:
- Precursor manufacturing.
- Active material manufacturing.
- Cell manufacturing.
- Battery assembly.

If you buy cells from a supplier, *they* must provide you with their primary data. This requires establishing secure, B2B data pipelines immediately.

### 3. The Secondary Data Penalty
For background processes (like generic transport or the mining of non-critical trace minerals), you are permitted to use Secondary Data from an EU-approved database.

However, the JRC draft rules introduce a crucial mechanism: **The Penalty Factor**. If you fail to obtain primary data for a mandatory foreground process, you are forced to use secondary data with a heavy penalty applied. This artificially inflates your carbon score, putting your battery at risk of missing the 2028 maximum carbon thresholds.

## Navigating Guarantees of Origin (GOs)

Many gigafactories claim "100% renewable energy." Under the JRC rules, claiming this requires strict proof.

If you are not physically wired directly to a solar/wind farm (behind-the-meter), you must use Guarantees of Origin (GOs) or Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs). The JRC rules demand **strict temporal and geographical correlation**:
- You can only claim solar energy during the hours the sun is shining.
- The renewable energy must be generated on the same interconnected electricity grid as your factory.

## What to do next

You cannot wait until 2025 to figure this out.
1. Ask your current LCA software vendor if they explicitly support the latest JRC PEFCR drafts for batteries.
2. Send formal data requests to your cathode and anode suppliers demanding primary energy data.
