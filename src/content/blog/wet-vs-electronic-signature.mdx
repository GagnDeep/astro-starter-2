As businesses move toward paperless operations, understanding the terminology of signatures is crucial.

## What is a Wet Signature?

A **wet signature** is a physical mark made on paper using ink. It's the traditional method of signing a document.

* **Pros:** Universally understood, inherently tied to the physical document.
* **Cons:** Slow, requires physical presence or mailing, expensive (see our [Paper Cost Calculator](/tools/paper-cost-calculator)), and easily forged without an expert.

## What is an Electronic Signature?

An **electronic signature** (or e-signature) is a digital mark indicating intent to agree to a contract. This can be a typed name, a drawn image, or a clicked button.

* **Pros:** Instant, trackable via [Audit Trails](/security/audit-trails), highly secure, and legally binding under the [ESIGN Act](/legality/us-esign-act).
* **Cons:** Requires a basic level of digital literacy.

## What about Digital Signatures?

It's important not to confuse electronic signatures with *digital signatures*. A digital signature is a specific, highly secure subset of e-signatures that uses Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) to encrypt the document and tightly verify the signer's identity via a Certificate Authority.

For 99% of small business needs (NDAs, freelance contracts, B2B agreements), a standard electronic signature provided by tools like [usignpdf](/) is perfectly sufficient and legally binding.
