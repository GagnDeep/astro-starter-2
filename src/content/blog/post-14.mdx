Mac users have a distinct advantage when it comes to PDFs: Apple's built-in Preview app is remarkably capable. However, it isn't perfect for every workflow. Let's look at the best options for annotating PDFs on macOS in 2024.

## 1. Apple Preview (The Built-in Option)

**Pros:**
* Already installed.
* Fast and integrates with the OS.
* Excellent at basic highlighting and signatures.

**Cons:**
* Can sometimes corrupt complex [PDF Forms](/reference/topic-18).
* Sharing annotated drafts requires saving and emailing files back and forth.

## 2. uViewPDF (The Browser-Based Option)

For users who want a friction-free experience without worrying about desktop file management, [uViewPDF's Annotation Tool](/tools/annotate-pdf) is a strong alternative.

**Pros:**
* **OS Agnostic:** Works exactly the same on Mac, Windows, or Linux.
* **Privacy:** Processes locally in your browser, keeping [encrypted files](/reference/topic-2) secure.
* **No installation:** Bookmark the page and you're done.

## 3. Foxit Reader for Mac (The Heavyweight Option)

**Pros:**
* Massive feature set matching premium software.
* Deep integration with enterprise systems.

**Cons:**
* Very heavy installer.
* Cluttered interface can be overwhelming for simple tasks.
* Will try to upsell you to the paid version constantly.

### The Verdict

If you just need to sign a document quickly, use Apple Preview. If you need to markup a contract and share specific pages (see [how to extract pages](/guides/guide-2)), a lightweight web tool is significantly faster.
