One of the most confusing things for a beginner is terminology. You'll hear the terms "mutual fund" and "index fund" thrown around constantly. Often, they are used interchangeably, which is incorrect.

Here is the easiest way to understand it:
**A mutual fund is how the money is packaged.**
**An index fund is how the money is managed.**

In fact, an index fund *is a type* of mutual fund (or ETF).

### What is a Mutual Fund?
A mutual fund is simply a pool of money collected from many investors to invest in securities like stocks or bonds.

Historically, almost all mutual funds were **actively managed**. This means there is a very smart (and very expensive) portfolio manager sitting in an office, researching companies, and deciding which stocks to buy and sell in order to "beat the market."

Because you have to pay the manager, their research team, and their trading costs, actively managed mutual funds have high fees (expense ratios), often around 1.0% to 1.5% per year.

### What is an Index Fund?
An index fund is a fund that is **passively managed**.

There is no manager trying to pick winners. Instead, the fund simply buys a little bit of everything in a specific mathematical index (like the S&P 500, which is the 500 largest US companies). If a company is in the index, the fund buys it. If it falls out of the index, the fund sells it.

Because a computer algorithm can run an index fund, the costs are incredibly low. You can easily find index funds with fees as low as 0.03% per year.

### Why Index Funds Usually Win

You might think, "I'll gladly pay a 1% fee if the smart manager can get me a 15% return instead of the market's 10% return."

The problem is, they can't.

According to the S&P Indices Versus Active (SPIVA) scorecard, over a 15-year period, **nearly 90% of actively managed mutual funds underperform their benchmark index.**

Think about that. Wall Street professionals, with Ivy League degrees and Bloomberg terminals, fail to beat a simple, brainless index fund 9 times out of 10 over the long term.

Furthermore, you don't know *which* 10% of managers will succeed in the future. The manager who beats the market this year is highly likely to underperform it next year.

### The Verdict

Active mutual funds try to find the needle in the haystack. They charge you a lot of money to do it, and they usually fail.

Index funds just buy the entire haystack. They charge you almost nothing, and they guarantee you will get exactly the market's return.

For the average investor, buying the haystack (via a low-cost index fund) is the undisputed best strategy.
