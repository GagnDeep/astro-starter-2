Purchasing real estate in the Cayman Islands involves a significant closing cost: a 7.5% Stamp Duty levied by the government. For a CI$600,000 home, that is an additional CI$45,000 required in cash at closing.

To help locals get onto the property ladder in an increasingly expensive market, the government offers substantial concessions—but only for **Caymanian citizens** purchasing their first home.

## The Current Concession Tiers (2024)

If you hold Caymanian Status and do not currently own property in the Cayman Islands, you are eligible for the following waivers:

### Existing Homes or Apartments
*   **Purchase Price up to CI$400,000:** The stamp duty is completely waived (0%). You pay nothing.
*   **Purchase Price between CI$400,001 and CI$500,000:** You pay a reduced concessionary rate of **2%** only on the portion of the price that exceeds CI$400,000.
*   **Purchase Price above CI$500,000:** No concession applies. You pay the full 7.5% on the entire purchase price.

### Raw Land (Bare Land)
*   **Purchase Price up to CI$150,000:** Completely waived (0%).
*   **Purchase Price between CI$150,001 and CI$200,000:** Reduced rate of 2% on the difference.
*   **Purchase Price above CI$200,000:** No concession applies.

## The Joint Purchase Dilemma

A frequent point of confusion arises when a Caymanian buys a property jointly with a non-Caymanian (often a spouse on a work permit).
*   In this scenario, the concession is applied proportionally. The Caymanian purchaser's 50% share of the property benefits from the waiver/reduction, while the non-Caymanian's 50% share is assessed at the full 7.5% rate.

## The "Bare Land" Strategy

Many first-time buyers use the raw land concession as a strategic entry point. They purchase a plot of land for under CI$150,000 (paying zero stamp duty) and then build a home on it later.

Because stamp duty is assessed at the time of the land transfer, you only pay duty on the value of the raw land, not on the value of the house you subsequently build. This can result in massive savings compared to buying a pre-built home.

*Note: Expats on work permits are entirely excluded from these concessions and must pay the full 7.5% regardless of purchase price.*
