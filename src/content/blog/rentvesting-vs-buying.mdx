# Rentvesting vs Buying a Home: A Data-Driven Comparison

For decades, the "Great Australian Dream" dictated a strict path: save a deposit, buy a home to live in, pay it off, and perhaps invest later. However, with housing affordability at historic lows in major capital cities, a new strategy has emerged: [Rentvesting](/glossary/rentvesting).

Rentvesting involves renting a property to live in (usually in a desirable, expensive location) while simultaneously purchasing an investment property in a more affordable area. But is it mathematically superior to buying a Principal Place of Residence (PPOR)? Let's analyze the data.

## The Case for Buying a Home (PPOR)

The traditional approach has significant psychological and financial benefits, primarily driven by tax legislation.

### Financial Advantages of a PPOR
1. **Capital Gains Tax Exemption:** This is the most powerful wealth-creation tool available to Australians. Any capital growth on your primary residence is 100% tax-free. If you buy a house for $800,000 and sell it for $1.5 million ten years later, that $700,000 profit is yours to keep, entirely untouched by the ATO.
2. **Forced Savings:** Paying a mortgage forces financial discipline. Every principal payment increases your [Equity](/glossary/equity).
3. **Stability and Control:** You are not subject to landlord decisions, lease non-renewals, or arbitrary rent increases. You can renovate and modify the home as you see fit.

### The Drawbacks
*   **The "Dead Money" Myth:** While renters are told rent is "dead money," homeowners face unrecoverable costs too: interest payments, council rates, maintenance, and insurance. These are not tax-deductible on a PPOR.
*   **Location Compromise:** Unless you have a very high income, buying a PPOR often means compromising on location, pushing buyers to outer suburbs far from lifestyle amenities and employment hubs.

## The Case for Rentvesting

Rentvesting decouples *where you live* from *where you invest*, optimizing for both lifestyle and financial return.

### Financial Advantages of Rentvesting
1. **Tax Deductibility:** This is the core engine of rentvesting. While the rent you pay is a sunk cost, the costs of holding your investment property (interest, rates, maintenance, property management, and depreciation) are all tax-deductible against your income. This can significantly reduce holding costs via [Negative Gearing](/glossary/negative-gearing).
2. **Lower Barrier to Entry:** You can enter the property market sooner. If you live in Sydney but can only afford a $500,000 property, you can rent in Sydney and invest in Adelaide, Perth, or regional centers where $500,000 buys a house with strong [Capital Growth](/glossary/capital-growth) potential. (Read: [How Much Deposit Do I Really Need?](/blog/how-much-deposit-do-i-really-need)).
3. **Yield Discrepancy:** In expensive inner-city areas, rental yields are often very low (e.g., 2-3%). It is mathematically cheaper to rent a $1.5 million house than it is to pay the mortgage, rates, and maintenance on it. You can deploy your capital into markets where the yield is higher (e.g., 5-6%), making your money work harder.

### The Drawbacks
*   **Capital Gains Tax (CGT):** When you sell your investment property, you will pay [Capital Gains Tax](/glossary/capital-gains-tax). Even with the 50% discount for holding the asset for more than 12 months, this significantly reduces your net profit compared to the PPOR exemption.
*   **Lack of Stability:** You are at the mercy of the rental market. You may be forced to move, and you generally cannot modify the property.

## A Worked Example: The Numbers

*Assumptions: An investor has a $100,000 deposit and a $120,000 salary.*

**Scenario A: Buy PPOR in Outer Suburbs**
*   Buys an $800,000 home (requires LMI).
*   Mortgage: $720,000 at 6%. Interest = $43,200/year.
*   Rates/Maintenance: $5,000/year.
*   **Unrecoverable Costs:** ~$48,200/year.
*   *Tax Deductions:* $0.
*   *Capital Gains Tax on Sale:* $0.

**Scenario B: Rentvesting**
*   Rents an inner-city apartment for $650/week ($33,800/year).
*   Buys a $600,000 investment property in a high-growth regional center.
*   Mortgage: $540,000 at 6.2% (investment rates are slightly higher). Interest = $33,480/year.
*   Investment Expenses (Rates, PM, Maintenance): $7,000/year.
*   Investment Rental Income: $450/week ($23,400/year).
*   *Gross Shortfall on Investment:* $17,080/year.
*   *Tax Refund (assuming 37% bracket + Medicare):* ~$6,600/year.
*   *Net Holding Cost of Investment:* $10,480/year.
*   **Total Unrecoverable Costs (Rent + Net Holding Cost):** ~$44,280/year.
*   *Capital Gains Tax on Sale:* Applicable.

*Use our [Holding Costs Calculator](/tools/holding-costs-calculator) to model the investment portion of this scenario.*

### The Verdict on the Numbers
In the short term, rentvesting (Scenario B) may actually result in lower annual cash outflow ($44k vs $48k), while providing a superior lifestyle. However, over a 15-year period, the tax-free nature of the PPOR's capital growth (Scenario A) often results in a higher net wealth figure upon sale, *unless* the rentvestor's chosen investment market significantly outperforms the outer-suburb PPOR market.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I use the First Home Owner Grant if I rentvest?**
A: Generally, no. Most government grants and stamp duty concessions require you to live in the property as your primary residence for a continuous period (usually 6-12 months). However, some rentvestors buy a property, live in it for the required period to claim the grants, and then move out and turn it into an investment.

**Q: What if rent prices spike?**
A: This is a major risk for rentvestors. If the rent on your home increases dramatically, your cash flow is squeezed. Conversely, if interest rates rise, the PPOR owner feels the squeeze. Both strategies carry inflation risk.

## Conclusion

Rentvesting is not a magic bullet, nor is it strictly superior to buying a home.
Choose Rentvesting if: You prioritize lifestyle location, want to enter the market with a smaller deposit, and are comfortable managing the lack of housing stability and future CGT liabilities.
Choose PPOR if: You prioritize stability, forced savings, and the immense power of tax-free capital growth over a 10-20 year horizon.
