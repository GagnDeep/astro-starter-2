In a significant victory for the jurisdiction's financial services industry, the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) formally removed the Cayman Islands from its "grey list" of jurisdictions under increased monitoring in late October 2023.

This decision validates years of legislative reform, increased enforcement action, and significant investment in regulatory bodies by the Cayman Islands government.

## What Does This Mean for Business?

For investment funds, asset managers, and corporate entities structured in Cayman, the removal eliminates friction in cross-border transactions. During the grey-listing period, some institutional investors and European financial institutions applied enhanced due diligence (EDD) to Cayman entities, slowing down capital flows and increasing compliance costs.

With the jurisdiction now recognized as fully compliant with global AML/CFT standards, structuring in Cayman regains its undisputed status as the preeminent offshore jurisdiction.

## The Road to Removal

To achieve this, the Cayman Islands successfully addressed all recommended actions in its FATF action plan, specifically:

1.  Demonstrating effective application of sanctions for AML/CFT breaches.
2.  Ensuring that beneficial ownership information is accurate, adequate, and up-to-date.
3.  Proving that money laundering prosecutions result in effective, proportionate, and dissuasive sanctions.

The FATF plenary in Paris noted that the Cayman Islands' AML/CFT regime is now highly effective and resilient.
