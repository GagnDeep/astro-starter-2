When you're a freelancer or a small agency, sending a contract shouldn't feel like navigating enterprise software. Yet, that's exactly what happens when you use DocuSign.

In this guide, we break down why small businesses are looking for DocuSign alternatives and present the best options available in 2024.

## The Problem with DocuSign for Small Teams

DocuSign is undeniably the 800lb gorilla of the e-signature world. However, it's built for enterprise workflows.

**Common frustrations include:**
1. **Pricing:** At $30/month for a standard plan, it's expensive if you only send a few contracts a month.
2. **Complexity:** The UI is cluttered with features you don't need (complex routing, CRM integrations).
3. **Friction for Clients:** Sometimes forces clients into a confusing account-creation flow.

## Top Alternatives

### 1. usignpdf

We built [usignpdf](/) specifically to solve these problems. It's the frictionless, instant way to sign PDFs.
- **Pros:** Zero client signup, instant drop-zone interface, free tier available, ironclad audit trails.
- **Cons:** No advanced CRM integrations (by design).

### 2. HelloSign (Dropbox Sign)

HelloSign is a solid alternative, though it has moved upmarket since being acquired by Dropbox.
- **Pros:** Clean UI, good API.
- **Cons:** Pricing is still high for solo users, pushes you into the Dropbox ecosystem.

### 3. PandaDoc

Great if you need to build proposals, not just sign PDFs.
- **Pros:** Excellent proposal editor, CPQ features.
- **Cons:** Overkill if you just need to sign an existing PDF contract.

## Which should you choose?

If you want to create complex proposals and track when clients open them, look at PandaDoc.

If you just have a PDF (like an NDA or a standard contract) and need it signed *right now* with zero hassle, use [usignpdf](/).
