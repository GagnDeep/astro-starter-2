We've all been there. You're waiting in line for coffee, and an urgent email comes in: *"Can you sign and return this NDA right now so we can kick off the project?"*

Historically, this meant rushing back to your laptop, opening preview, or worse—finding a printer. Today, signing a PDF on your iPhone should take less than 30 seconds.

In this guide, we'll cover the built-in iOS method, its limitations, and the better, legally robust way to do it.

## Method 1: The Built-in iOS Markup Tool (Quick, but risky)

Apple has built a markup tool directly into the Mail and Files apps.

**How to do it:**
1. Open the email containing the PDF.
2. Tap the PDF attachment to open it.
3. Tap the **Markup icon** (a small pen tip in the top right corner).
4. Tap the **"+" button** in the bottom right corner.
5. Select **Signature**.
6. If you've previously saved a signature, tap it. Otherwise, use your finger to draw one.
7. Drag the signature to the correct line, resize it, and tap **Done**.
8. Select "Reply All" to send it back.

### The Problem with iOS Markup

While convenient, this method has a massive flaw for B2B contracts: **It has no Audit Trail.**

When you use iOS Markup, you are simply flattening an image of your signature onto the PDF. If a dispute arises, there is no cryptographic proof that *you* were the one who placed it there. It lacks [non-repudiation](/glossary#non-repudiation). Furthermore, it provides no [document integrity](/glossary#document-integrity)—the recipient could easily alter the text of the PDF after you send it back.
