The Cayman Islands Department for International Tax Cooperation (DITC) has released the 2024 deadline schedule for the Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI). All Cayman Islands Financial Institutions (FIs)—including investment funds, banks, and certain trust companies—must strictly adhere to these dates to avoid severe administrative penalties.

## The 2024 AEOI Deadlines

### CRS and FATCA Registration
*   **Deadline: April 30, 2024**
*   **Requirement:** Any new entity that became a Cayman Reporting Financial Institution must register on the DITC Portal and notify the Tax Information Authority (TIA) by this date. For FATCA, this implies having already secured a Global Intermediary Identification Number (GIIN) from the US IRS.

### CRS Reporting (XML Upload)
*   **Deadline: July 31, 2024**
*   **Requirement:** FIs must submit their CRS XML returns via the DITC Portal containing financial account information for reportable accounts for the 2023 calendar year.

### FATCA Reporting (XML Upload)
*   **Deadline: July 31, 2024**
*   **Requirement:** FIs must submit their FATCA XML returns via the DITC Portal.

### CRS Compliance Form
*   **Deadline: September 15, 2024**
*   **Requirement:** Every Cayman Reporting FI must submit the CRS Compliance Form. This form requires the FI to confirm it has established written AEOI policies and procedures and to provide data on the volume of accounts reviewed.

## Why the CRS Compliance Form Matters

Introduced a few years ago, the CRS Compliance Form represents a shift in the TIA's approach from data collection to active enforcement. By forcing an FI's principal point of contact (usually a director) to sign off on the existence of written procedures, the TIA creates a clear paper trail for liability if an audit later reveals those procedures were inadequate or non-existent.

## The Cost of Non-Compliance

The DITC is no longer in the "educational" phase of AEOI implementation. The enforcement framework is robust:
*   Failure to register: CI$10,000 fine.
*   Failure to file a return by the deadline: CI$5,000 fine.
*   Inaccurate reporting or failure to establish policies: Fines can escalate rapidly up to CI$50,000.

## Common Pitfalls

1.  **Waiting for the GIIN:** You cannot complete DITC registration without a GIIN. The IRS portal can sometimes take weeks to process a GIIN application, causing funds to miss the April 30th DITC deadline.
2.  **Nil Returns:** If an FI has no reportable accounts, it *must still file a Nil Return*. Failing to file a Nil Return triggers the same late-filing penalty as failing to file a substantive return.
3.  **Entity Classification Errors:** Many holding companies incorrectly classify themselves as Active Non-Financial Entities (NFEs) to avoid reporting. The DITC actively audits entity classifications, particularly for entities within broader fund structures.
