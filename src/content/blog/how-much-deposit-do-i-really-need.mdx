# How Much Deposit Do I Really Need for an Investment Property?

A common misconception holding many potential investors back is the belief that they need a 20% cash deposit to enter the market. While a 20% deposit is the gold standard for avoiding extra fees, the reality is that lenders offer far more flexibility, provided you understand the risks and costs involved.

Let's break down the data on deposit requirements, the impact of Lenders Mortgage Insurance, and alternative ways to fund your purchase.

## The 20% Benchmark and LVR

When assessing a loan, banks use a metric called Loan-to-Value Ratio ([LVR](/glossary/lvr)). If you buy a $500,000 property with a $100,000 deposit, your loan is $400,000, which equates to an 80% LVR.

An LVR of 80% or below is the traditional threshold where lenders feel secure. At this level, you will not be required to pay Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI).

### Why Aim for a 20% Deposit?
1. **Avoid LMI:** LMI is an expensive premium that protects the lender, not you.
2. **Lower Interest Rates:** Lenders often reserve their most competitive interest rates for borrowers with lower LVRs (typically &lt;70% or &lt;80%).
3. **Better Cash Flow:** A smaller loan means lower interest repayments, improving your [Holding Costs](/glossary/holding-costs).
4. **Buffer Against Market Dips:** If property values fall by 10%, an investor with an 80% LVR still has 10% equity. An investor with a 95% LVR would be in negative equity (owing more than the property is worth).

## Buying with Less Than 20%: The Reality of LMI

If you don't have a 20% deposit, you can still buy an investment property, often with deposits as low as 10% (and occasionally 5% in specific circumstances, though stricter rules usually apply to investment loans compared to owner-occupier loans).

However, you will incur [LMI](/glossary/lmi).

### How Much Does LMI Cost?
LMI premiums scale aggressively based on two factors: the size of the loan and the LVR percentage. (Data Source: Genworth / Helia LMI estimators).

*Example: Purchasing a $600,000 Investment Property*

| Deposit Size | Deposit Amount | LVR | Estimated LMI Premium |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 20% | $120,000 | 80% | $0 |
| 15% | $90,000 | 85% | ~$6,500 |
| 10% | $60,000 | 90% | ~$13,000 |
| 5% | $30,000 | 95% | ~$22,000 (often not available for investors) |

*Note: Many lenders allow you to capitalize the LMI (add it to the loan balance), so you don't need to pay it as a cash lump sum upfront. However, this means you pay interest on the LMI premium over the life of the loan.*

### Is Paying LMI Worth It?
Sometimes, yes. In a rising market, the opportunity cost of waiting 3 years to save a 20% deposit might be greater than the cost of the LMI.

If a $600,000 property grows at 6% annually, it gains $36,000 in value in the first year. Paying a $13,000 LMI premium to enter the market *now* might be mathematically sound compared to missing out on that growth while saving. (See our analysis on [Capital Growth vs Rental Yield](/blog/capital-growth-vs-rental-yield)).

## Funding the Deposit: Alternative Strategies

If saving cash is taking too long, data-driven investors look at alternative funding methods.

### 1. Using Existing Equity
If you already own a home or another investment property, you may have "useable equity." Banks generally allow you to borrow up to 80% of your existing property's value, minus the current mortgage balance.

*Example:*
* Your home is worth $800,000.
* 80% of that is $640,000.
* Your current mortgage is $400,000.
* You have **$240,000** in useable equity that can be extracted to form the deposit and cover purchasing costs for a new investment property.

*Calculate your exact position using our [Useable Equity Calculator](/tools/equity-calculator).* (Also read our deep dive on [Using Equity to Buy](/library/using-equity-to-buy)).

### 2. Cross-Collateralisation (A Warning)
Banks may suggest [Cross-Collateralisation](/glossary/cross-collateralisation), where they tie multiple properties together under one loan structure. While it simplifies the deposit process (you don't have to extract equity as cash), it severely limits your flexibility, makes selling individual assets complicated, and gives the bank total control over your portfolio. Most strategic investors prefer standalone loan structures.

### 3. Rentvesting
If you can't afford a deposit in the area you want to live, [Rentvesting](/glossary/rentvesting) allows you to rent where you want, and buy an investment property in a more affordable location where a smaller deposit is required. (Read more: [Rentvesting vs Buying a Home](/blog/rentvesting-vs-buying)).

## Don't Forget the Purchasing Costs
The deposit is not the only cash you need. As outlined in our [Hidden Costs of Buying](/blog/hidden-costs-of-buying-investment-property) guide, you must budget for Stamp Duty, legal fees, and building inspections. On a $600,000 property, these costs can add another $25,000 to the total cash required at settlement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I use a personal loan for the deposit?**
A: Generally, no. Lenders require "genuine savings." They want to see that you have the financial discipline to accumulate funds over time. Using a personal loan increases your overall debt obligations, which severely reduces your borrowing capacity.

**Q: What is the absolute minimum I need?**
A: Practically, for an investment property, you need a 10% deposit PLUS enough cash to cover stamp duty and legal fees. For a $500,000 property, expect to need around $70,000 in cash or useable equity to comfortably complete the transaction.

## Conclusion

You do not explicitly need a 20% deposit to invest in property, but buying with less requires accepting LMI and slightly higher interest rates. The decision should be based on mathematical modeling: compare the cost of LMI against the potential capital growth you might miss while attempting to save a larger deposit.
