# CalyxOS vs GrapheneOS: Which Privacy Phone is Right for You?

The debate of "calyxos vs grapheneos" is arguably the most common discussion in the privacy community. Both operating systems target Google Pixel devices (and a few others), and both aim to remove Google's pervasive tracking from your daily life. However, their underlying philosophies, threat models, and technical implementations are wildly different.

## The Philosophical Difference

*   **CalyxOS (Usability First):** Developed by the non-profit Calyx Institute, CalyxOS focuses primarily on "de-googling" while maintaining maximum usability for the average user. It aims to make a privacy-respecting phone feel exactly like a normal Android phone, just without the data harvesting.
*   **GrapheneOS (Security First):** [GrapheneOS](/os/grapheneos/) focuses on deep system hardening and security against advanced adversaries. See our [Pegasus vs GrapheneOS](/blog/pegasus-vs-grapheneos/) article for more details. Privacy is achieved as a byproduct of this intense, uncompromising security model. It expects users to adapt to its constraints.

## microG vs Sandboxed Google Play

This is the technical heart of the debate and the main driver of app compatibility differences.

### The CalyxOS Approach: microG

CalyxOS ships with **microG** integrated at the system level. [microG](/glossary/microg/) is an open-source, reverse-engineered reimplementation of Google Play Services. It spoofs Google's APIs, allowing apps that require Google Services (like push notifications or location APIs) to function without actually sending your data to Google.

*   **Pros:** Completely open-source, completely removes proprietary Google code from the OS layer, highly private.
*   **Cons:** Because it's a spoof, it occasionally breaks when Google updates their APIs. It cannot pass strict SafetyNet or Play Integrity checks, meaning apps like Google Pay or high-security banking apps will often refuse to run.

### The GrapheneOS Approach: Sandboxed Google Play

GrapheneOS firmly rejects microG. Instead, they built a sophisticated compatibility layer that allows the **real Google Play Services** to run as a standard, unprivileged app.

*   **Pros:** App compatibility is nearly 100%. Because it's the real code, push notifications and complex apps (like Uber or banking apps) work flawlessly.
*   **Cons:** You are running proprietary Google code on your phone. However, because it's strictly sandboxed, Google has no special access to your phone's IMEI, hardware identifiers, or other apps.

| Feature | CalyxOS | GrapheneOS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Google Services** | microG (Open Source Spoof) | Sandboxed Play (Sandboxed Proprietary) |
| **App Compatibility** | High (Breaks on strict DRM/Banking) | Very High (Almost everything works) |
| **Default Security** | Standard AOSP + some hardening | Extreme hardening (hardened allocator, exec spawning limits) |
| **Target Audience** | Everyday users wanting to de-google | Journalists, activists, security professionals |
| **Device Support** | Pixels, Fairphone 4, Moto Edge 30 | Exclusively Google Pixels |

## Battery Life

Battery life is a common concern. In standard usage, both operating systems offer excellent battery life, typically 15-20% better than stock Android because they lack the constant background telemetry phoning home to Google.

However, edge cases exist. On CalyxOS, if an app aggressively polls for Google Services that microG hasn't perfectly replicated, it can cause battery drain. On GrapheneOS, if Sandboxed Play Services constantly crashes in the background due to missing permissions, it can also drain the battery. Generally, if configured correctly, both offer superior endurance to stock.

## Which Should You Choose?

If you want a phone that "just works" out of the box, you philosophically refuse to run any proprietary Google code, and you don't rely on strict banking apps or Google Pay, choose **CalyxOS**.

If you want the absolute highest level of security against zero-days, and require strict compatibility with banking apps or ride-sharing apps (while being okay with sandboxed proprietary code), choose **GrapheneOS**.
