For entities registered in the Cayman Islands, the Economic Substance (ES) framework is one of the most critical compliance hurdles. At the heart of this framework is the requirement to conduct **Core Income Generating Activities (CIGA)** on the island.

## What is CIGA?

If an entity conducts a "relevant activity" (such as fund management, headquarters business, or intellectual property business), it must demonstrate that the activities central to generating profit from that business are actually performed in the Cayman Islands.

It is no longer acceptable to have a Cayman entity that simply acts as a booking center while all the strategic decision-making and operational execution happens in London or New York.

## Examples of CIGA by Industry

The Economic Substance Act defines specific CIGAs for different relevant activities:

*   **Fund Management Business:** Taking decisions on the holding and selling of investments; calculating risks and reserves; taking decisions on currency or interest fluctuations and hedging positions; preparing relevant regulatory or other reports for government authorities and investors.
*   **Headquarters Business:** Taking relevant management decisions; incurring expenditures on behalf of group entities; coordinating group activities.
*   **Shipping Business:** Managing the crew (including hiring, paying, and overseeing members); hauling and maintaining ships; overseeing and tracking deliveries; determining what goods to order and when to deliver them.

## The Outsourcing Dilemma

A common question is whether a Cayman entity can outsource its CIGA.

The short answer is **yes, but only locally**.

An entity can outsource its CIGA to a service provider (like a corporate services firm or a local investment manager), provided that the service provider performs those activities *within the Cayman Islands*. The entity must also be able to demonstrate that it adequately supervises the outsourced activities.

Outsourcing CIGA to a firm outside of the Cayman Islands will result in an immediate failure of the Economic Substance test.

## Consequences of Failing the CIGA Test

The Tax Information Authority (TIA) rigorously audits ES returns. If an entity is found to lack substance (i.e., it is not performing CIGA on-island relative to the income it generates), the consequences are severe:

1.  **Initial Fine:** CI$10,000.
2.  **Subsequent Fine:** If the failure is not rectified in the following year, the fine jumps to CI$100,000.
3.  **Information Exchange:** The TIA is legally obligated to notify the tax authorities in the jurisdictions where the entity's ultimate parent and beneficial owners reside.
4.  **Strike-off:** Persistent failure can lead to the entity being struck off the Cayman Islands company register.
