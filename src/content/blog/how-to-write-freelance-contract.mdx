Writing a freelance contract doesn't require a law degree, but missing key clauses can cost you thousands of dollars.

In this guide, we'll walk through exactly what needs to be in your contract and how to get it signed instantly using [usignpdf](/).

## 1. Scope of Work (SOW)

The most important part of any contract is defining exactly what you will do. If you are vague here, you invite scope creep.

- **Bad:** "Build a website."
- **Good:** "Design and develop a 5-page WordPress website including Home, About, Services, Blog, and Contact, with one round of revisions."

## 2. Payment Terms

Don't just state the total amount. Detail *when* you get paid.

- 50% deposit upfront (non-refundable).
- 50% upon final delivery.
- Net-15 or Net-30 terms for the final invoice.

## 3. Revisions and Kill Fees

What happens if the client changes their mind?
- Clearly state how many revisions are included (usually 1-2).
- Include a "Kill Fee": If they cancel the project halfway through, you retain the deposit and are owed for work completed to date.

## Example Contract Structure

| Section | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Parties** | Who is agreeing to what. |
| **Scope** | What exactly are you delivering? |
| **Timeline** | When are milestones due? |
| **Payment** | How much, and when? |
| **Ownership** | Who owns the IP after payment? |
