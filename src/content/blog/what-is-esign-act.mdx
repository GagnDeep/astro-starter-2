If you've ever wondered, *"Is this digital signature actually legally binding?"*, the answer usually traces back to the ESIGN Act.

## A Brief History

Before 2000, courts were split on whether an electronic signature carried the same weight as a physical "wet signature." To standardize commerce in the digital age, the US federal government passed the **Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (ESIGN)** in 2000.

## The Core Principle

The ESIGN Act states a simple but powerful rule: **A signature, contract, or other record relating to such transaction may not be denied legal effect, validity, or enforceability solely because it is in electronic form.**

In short: electronic signatures are legal signatures.

## 4 Requirements for Compliance

To be protected under the ESIGN Act, an electronic signature must meet four criteria:

1. **Intent to Sign:** The signer must show clear intent (e.g., typing their name, drawing a signature, or clicking a button).
2. **Consent to do Business Electronically:** The consumer must consent to sign electronically.
3. **Association of Signature:** The system must record how the signature was created (this is why usignpdf provides an [Audit Trail](/security/audit-trails)).
4. **Record Retention:** The document must be saved and accessible to both parties.

## Are there exceptions?

Yes. The ESIGN act does not apply to certain documents, such as:
- Wills and testamentary trusts
- Family law documents (divorce, adoption)
- Court orders and official court documents
