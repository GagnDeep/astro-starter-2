If you've ever searched for a "free PDF editor," you know the frustration. You upload your document, spend 20 minutes carefully adjusting text and adding highlights, only to hit "download" and find a massive watermark plastered across your page.

It's a bait-and-switch that forces you into a paid subscription just to get a clean file.

## Why do tools add watermarks?

Providing server-side processing for PDFs is expensive. Companies use watermarks as a paywall mechanism to recoup those costs. However, modern web browsers are incredibly powerful and can process PDFs locally, eliminating those server costs.

## The Solution: Local Processing

By using an [online annotator](/tools/annotate-pdf) that leverages your browser's local resources rather than a cloud server, you can add text, highlights, and drawings to your PDF completely free, with absolutely **no watermarks**.

### Step-by-Step

1. Navigate to the [Annotation Tool](/tools/annotate-pdf).
2. Drag and drop your file.
3. Select the "Text" tool from the menu.
4. Click anywhere on the document to start typing.
5. Click "Save". The file downloads instantly, watermark-free.

### Edge Case: Editing *Existing* Text

It is important to understand a technical limitation dictated by [PDF Specifications](/reference/topic-1). Most "free" online tools do not actually edit existing text (like you would in Microsoft Word). They act as a layer *on top* of the PDF.

If you need to change a typo in a contract, the standard method is:
1. Draw a white box over the typo to hide it.
2. Type the correct text over the white box.

This is much faster and safer (see our [Security Guide](/reference/topic-2)) than converting the PDF to Word, editing it, and converting it back.
