When you buy a mutual fund or an ETF, the company managing the fund doesn't do it for charity. They charge a fee, which is called the **Expense Ratio**.

An expense ratio is expressed as an annual percentage.
* If a fund has a 1.0% expense ratio, they take $10 for every $1,000 you have invested, every single year.
* If a fund has a 0.04% expense ratio, they take $0.40 for every $1,000 you have invested, every single year.

To a beginner, 1.0% sounds incredibly small. In the context of compounding interest over decades, it is devastating.

### The Math of Missing Money

Let’s run a scenario using our [Expense Ratio Calculator](/tools/expense-ratio).

Imagine you invest $500 a month for 30 years.
Assume the market returns 8% per year before fees.

**Scenario A: The Active Fund (1.0% fee)**
* Your actual return is 7% (8% market - 1% fee).
* After 30 years, you have **$606,000**.

**Scenario B: The Index Fund (0.04% fee)**
* Your actual return is 7.96%.
* After 30 years, you have **$734,000**.

In this scenario, paying a 1% fee didn't cost you 1% of your final balance. **It cost you $128,000.**

### Why does a 1% fee take 17% of your money?

It all comes down to the math of compound interest.

The fee isn't taken just once on your initial deposit. It is taken *every single year* on your entire balance.

If the fund takes 1% this year, that is money that is permanently gone. It will not grow next year, or the year after that. You lose the 1%, *and* you lose decades of potential compound growth on that 1%.

### How to Find the Expense Ratio

Before you ever buy a fund, you must find its expense ratio.

1. Go to your brokerage (or Yahoo Finance/Google Finance).
2. Type in the ticker symbol (e.g., SPY, VOO, ARKK).
3. Look for the "Profile" or "Summary" tab.
4. Find the line item labeled "Expense Ratio" or "Net Expense Ratio".

### The Rule of Thumb

As a beginner building a core, long-term portfolio, you should strive to keep your expense ratios as close to zero as possible.

* **Excellent:** 0.00% to 0.10% (Most broad-market index funds)
* **Acceptable:** 0.11% to 0.30% (Some specialized index funds or target-date funds)
* **Too High:** Anything over 0.50% (You are almost certainly paying an active manager who will likely underperform the index anyway).

Don't give up hundreds of thousands of dollars to Wall Street. Check your expense ratios.
