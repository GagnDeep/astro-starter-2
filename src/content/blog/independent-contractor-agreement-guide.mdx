If you hire freelancers, or if you are a freelancer yourself, the Independent Contractor Agreement (ICA) is the foundation of your working relationship.

Getting this document wrong can lead to intellectual property disputes, unpaid invoices, or worst of all, misclassification penalties from the IRS.

In this guide, we break down what must be in an ICA and how to execute it efficiently.

## Employee vs. Independent Contractor

The primary purpose of an ICA is to firmly establish that the worker is *not* an employee.

If the IRS (or the equivalent tax authority in your country) decides you have misclassified an employee as a contractor, the penalties are severe.

**Key differentiators:**
- **Control:** Does the company dictate *how*, *when*, and *where* the work is done? (If yes, they might be an employee).
- **Financial Risk:** Does the contractor invest in their own equipment and bear the risk of loss? (If yes, they are likely a contractor).

## Essential Clauses in an ICA

### 1. The Relationship Clause
Explicitly state that the relationship is one of an independent contractor and client, not employer/employee, partnership, or joint venture.

### 2. Scope of Services
Define exactly what the contractor will do. Vagueness here leads to scope creep. (We cover this heavily in our [freelance contract guide](/blog/how-to-write-freelance-contract)).

### 3. Payment Terms
State the rate (hourly, flat fee, retainer) and the invoicing schedule. Will invoices be paid Net-15 or Net-30?

### 4. Intellectual Property (IP) Ownership
This is crucial. Unless explicitly stated in writing, the creator of a work owns the copyright. The contract must contain a "Work for Hire" clause or an assignment of rights to ensure the client actually owns the work they paid for.

### 5. Confidentiality (NDA)
Protect your trade secrets. Often, this is a separate document (see our [NDA template](/templates/nda)), but it can be included in the main ICA.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a W-9 as well?**
A: Yes, if you are a US business paying a US contractor more than $600 in a year, you need a W-9 on file to issue a 1099 form at tax time. You can use our [W-9 template](/templates/w9) and get it signed electronically alongside the ICA.

**Q: Are these contracts valid globally?**
A: Generally, yes. E-signatures are broadly accepted in the EU under [eIDAS](/legality/eu-eidas), in the UK, Canada, and Australia. However, the specific *terms* of the contract should be governed by the laws of a specific jurisdiction (e.g., "This agreement shall be governed by the laws of the State of Delaware").
