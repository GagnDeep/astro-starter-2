Because the Cayman Islands manufactures almost nothing locally, residents rely heavily on importing goods, mostly from the United States. To replace income tax, the government generates a massive portion of its revenue by charging import duties on goods arriving at the border.

## Standard Duty Rates

Customs duty is calculated on the **CIF value** (Cost, Insurance, and Freight). This means you pay duty not just on the price of the item, but also on the cost of shipping and insuring it to get it to the island.

*   **Standard Rate:** The default duty rate for most consumer goods (clothing, furniture, electronics) is **22%**.
*   **Luxury Goods:** Items like jewelry or high-end electronics can attract higher rates.
*   **Vehicles:** Duty on cars is sliding scale based on the value of the vehicle, ranging from 29.5% for standard cars up to 42% for luxury vehicles over CI$30,000 CIF value.
*   **Duty-Free Items:** A few specific items are duty-free, such as books, computers (sometimes requiring specific classification), and certain agricultural/solar equipment.

## How to Ship Goods (Freight Forwarding)

Most expats do not ship directly via FedEx or UPS due to the exorbitant cost. Instead, they use a **freight forwarder**.

1.  **The Miami Address:** When you sign up with a local freight forwarder (e.g., Sta-Mar, Sprint, IBC, or Mailboxes Etc.), they give you a physical shipping address in Miami, Florida.
2.  **Online Shopping:** You order items on Amazon, Target, or Wayfair and ship them to your Miami address (often enjoying free US domestic shipping).
3.  **Consolidation and Shipping:** The forwarder receives your packages in Miami, consolidates them onto a container ship (or cargo plane), and brings them to Grand Cayman. Ocean freight is significantly cheaper but takes about a week; air freight is faster but expensive.
4.  **Customs Clearance:** The forwarder usually acts as your customs broker. They will ask you to upload the original invoice. They calculate the 22% duty, add their own handling/freight fees, and clear the package through Customs on your behalf.

## Relocating: Shipping Your Household

If you are moving to the island for the first time, you have a one-time opportunity under the **Transfer of Residence** concession.

You can import your used household and personal effects (furniture, clothing, books) **duty-free** provided:
*   The goods are older than 6 months.
*   They are imported within 6 months of your arrival in the Cayman Islands.
*   You intend to reside in the islands for a period of not less than 12 months.

*Note: This exemption does NOT apply to motor vehicles. If you ship your used car to Cayman, you will pay full duty on its current appraised value.*

## The "Luggage Allowance"

If you travel off-island (e.g., a shopping trip to Miami), you have a personal duty-free allowance upon your return. Currently, residents can bring in up to CI$500 worth of personal goods duty-free per trip. Anything above that must be declared and the 22% duty paid at the airport customs desk.
