Opening a retail bank account in the Cayman Islands is a notorious pain point for new expatriates. Due to the jurisdiction's strict Anti-Money Laundering (AML) regulations, local retail banks subject individual applicants to a level of scrutiny that often surprises expats accustomed to instantaneous digital banking in their home countries.

## The Local Retail Banks

There are a handful of "Class A" retail banks that service the local population and resident expats:
1.  **Butterfield Bank**
2.  **Cayman National Bank (CNB)**
3.  **Scotiabank**
4.  **RBC Royal Bank**
5.  **FirstCaribbean International Bank (FCIB)**

*Note: The massive global banks operating in Cayman (like UBS or Julius Baer) are "Class B" banks or trust companies catering exclusively to high-net-worth wealth management and institutional funds. They do not offer local checking accounts or debit cards.*

## Required KYC Documentation

To open an account, you generally cannot just walk into a branch. You must make an appointment and provide extensive documentation. Expect to produce:

1.  **Notarized Passport Copy:** And often a secondary form of ID (driver's license).
2.  **Proof of Residential Address:** This is the biggest hurdle for new arrivals. Banks require a utility bill or lease agreement in your name. A letter from your employer or landlord is sometimes acceptable as a stopgap.
3.  **Proof of Employment:** A formal job letter stating your salary, role, and start date.
4.  **Source of Funds Declaration:** You must explain where the money going into the account comes from (usually your salary).
5.  **Reference Letter:** A reference letter from your previous onshore bank stating you have maintained an account in good standing.

*Tip: The process can take anywhere from 2 to 6 weeks. Many employers will help facilitate introductions to their corporate banking contacts to speed up the process for new hires.*

## Currency and Foreign Exchange

The Cayman Islands uses the Cayman Islands Dollar (CI$), but the US Dollar (US$) is accepted everywhere.
*   **The Peg:** The CI$ is permanently pegged to the US$ at a rate of CI$1.00 = US$1.20 (or US$1.00 = CI$0.80).
*   **Dual Accounts:** When you open an account, you will typically open both a CI$ and a US$ account linked to the same debit card.
*   **FX Fees:** When spending or transferring money, be mindful of the bank's internal spread. While the official peg is 0.80/1.20, retail banks usually sell US dollars at 0.84 and buy them at 0.80, taking a margin on every conversion.

## Digital Banking and Alternatives

Local retail banking technology in Cayman lags slightly behind North America and Europe. While all banks offer online portals and apps, features like instant peer-to-peer transfers (like Venmo or Zelle) do not exist locally, though wire transfers between local banks take 1-2 business days.

Due to the lack of Apple Pay/Google Pay integration with local Cayman debit cards, many expats maintain an onshore account (e.g., in the US or UK) and wire funds back periodically to cover international subscriptions and digital wallet spending.
