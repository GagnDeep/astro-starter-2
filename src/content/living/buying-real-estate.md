The Cayman Islands real estate market is robust, driven by a combination of high local salaries, international investment, and the lack of annual property taxes. For long-term expats, buying property is often the most significant financial decision made on the island.

## Foreign Ownership Rules

Unlike many Caribbean jurisdictions (such as the Bahamas or Bermuda), the Cayman Islands imposes **no restrictions on foreign ownership of real estate**. Non-residents and expatriates on work permits can buy, sell, and rent out property freely. There are no "Alien Landholding Licenses" required.

Furthermore, there is **no annual property tax** and **no capital gains tax** upon the sale of the property.

## Stamp Duty

The government generates revenue from real estate via Stamp Duty, a one-time tax paid by the buyer at the time of purchase.
*   **The Standard Rate:** 7.5% of the purchase price or the market value (whichever is higher).
*   **First-Time Caymanian Buyers:** Enjoy concessions. Purchases under CI$400,000 are entirely exempt, with a sliding scale up to CI$500,000. These concessions *do not* apply to expats on work permits.
*   **Chattels Exemption:** Stamp duty is assessed on the real estate, not the furniture. It is standard practice to deduct the value of the "chattels" (furniture and appliances) from the purchase price before calculating the 7.5% duty.

## The Buying Process (Conveyancing)

The process is generally straightforward and follows English common law principles.

1.  **Offer and Acceptance:** Made via a real estate agent (CIREBA members adhere to strict professional standards).
2.  **Offer to Purchase Agreement:** A legally binding document outlining the price, deposit, and conditions (e.g., subject to financing or valuation).
3.  **The Deposit:** Usually 10% of the purchase price, held in escrow by the seller's attorney or the real estate broker.
4.  **Conditions Precedent:** The buyer secures a mortgage and a property valuation (appraisal).
5.  **Closing:** The balance is paid, stamp duty is settled, and the Transfer of Land document is registered with the Lands and Survey Department.

**Legal Fees:** It is highly recommended to use a local conveyancing attorney. Legal fees generally range from 0.5% to 1% of the purchase price.

## Mortgages for Expats

Local retail banks provide mortgages to expats on work permits, but the lending criteria are strict.
*   **Down Payment:** While Caymanians can sometimes secure 100% financing, expats typically need a minimum down payment of 10% to 20%.
*   **Interest Rates:** Local mortgage rates are generally tied to the US Prime Rate plus a bank margin (usually 1% to 3%). Consequently, local mortgages are directly affected by US Federal Reserve rate hikes.
*   **Term Limit:** Banks will usually only amortize the mortgage up to the applicant's retirement age (typically 65).
*   **Work Permit Risk:** Banks factor in the risk of your work permit not being renewed. Some banks will only lend to expats who have been on the island for several years or who work in high-demand "Tier 1" professions.
