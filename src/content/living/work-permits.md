To live and work in the Cayman Islands, expatriates must possess a valid work permit. The process is employer-driven, meaning you must have a job offer before a permit application can be made.

## The Work Permit Process

The Department of WORC (Workforce Opportunities & Residency Cayman) handles immigration and employment authorization.

1.  **Advertising the Role:** Before applying for a full permit, the employer must advertise the position locally on the WORC JobsCayman portal for at least 14 days to ensure no qualified Caymanians are available.
2.  **Application Submission:** The employer submits the application. The employee must provide:
    *   A completed medical questionnaire (including HIV/VDRL tests and chest X-ray).
    *   A police clearance certificate from their home country or last place of residence.
    *   Passport copies and passport-sized photos.
3.  **Approval:** The employee must wait outside the Cayman Islands until the permit is approved (unless transitioning from a Temporary Work Permit).

## Types of Permits

*   **Temporary Work Permit (TWP):** Valid for up to 6 months. Often used while the full permit is being processed. Can sometimes be expedited.
*   **Grant of a Work Permit (Full):** Usually issued for 1 or 2 years, sometimes up to 3 or 5 years for certain senior roles.

## Fees and Costs

Work permit fees are paid by the employer (it is illegal to pass this cost to the employee). Fees vary drastically based on the industry and the level of the role.
*   Administrative roles: CI$1,000 - CI$3,000 annually.
*   Professional/Managerial (e.g., Accountants, Lawyers): CI$10,000 - CI$15,000+ annually.
*   Financial Services Executives: Can exceed CI$30,000 annually.

## Dependents

Expats holding a full work permit can apply to have their spouse and children added as dependents. However, WORC assesses the primary permit holder's salary to ensure they can financially support dependents. The general rule of thumb is a minimum salary of CI$3,500/month for one dependent, increasing with family size, though higher thresholds apply in practice given the cost of living.
