The Cayman Islands does not grant citizenship by birth (unless the parents are Caymanian) and does not offer "Golden Visas" for passive investment. For expatriates on work permits, the only standard path to remaining on the island long-term is obtaining Permanent Residency (PR) via the points system.

## The 8-Year Rule (The "Term Limit")

Expats are generally subject to a "term limit" of 9 years. If you do not obtain PR, you must leave the island for at least one year (the "rollover") before you can apply for a new work permit.

To avoid rollover, you must apply for Permanent Residency after you have been legally and ordinarily resident in the Cayman Islands for a continuous period of **eight years**.

## The Points System

PR is not guaranteed. Applications are assessed by the Caymanian Status & Permanent Residency Board using a strict points system. An applicant must score a minimum of **114 points** out of a possible 215.

Points are awarded across several heavily weighted categories:

### 1. Occupation (Max 30 points)
Points are allocated based on the perceived priority and need for your specific job in the local economy. Highly specialized roles (doctors, senior accountants, certain IT professionals) score higher than general administrative roles.

### 2. Salary / Income (Max 30 points)
The higher your salary, the more points you receive. The system is designed to reward expats who contribute significantly to the local economy. A salary over CI$120,000 generally secures the maximum points in this category.

### 3. Investment in Real Estate (Max 30 points)
This is often the deciding factor. Points are awarded based on the value of local real estate you own (or have invested in via a local company). Expats planning to stay long-term usually buy property precisely to secure these 30 points.
*   *Note: Points are awarded based on equity. If you have a CI$500k house but a CI$450k mortgage, you only have CI$50k of equity to count towards points.*

### 4. Age (Max 10 points)
Maximum points are awarded to applicants aged 25 to 35, decreasing as the applicant gets older.

### 5. History and Culture Test (Max 20 points)
Every applicant must sit a multiple-choice test on Cayman Islands history, culture, and society. You score one point for every correct answer (up to 20).

### 6. Community Service and Integration (Max 20 points)
Points are awarded for verified volunteer work, coaching local sports teams, or mentoring Caymanian youth.

### Deductions
The Board can deduct points for adverse factors, such as criminal convictions, health issues that may strain the local system, or failure to integrate into the community.

## Application Costs

Applying for PR is a massive financial undertaking.
*   **Application Fee:** CI$1,000 (non-refundable).
*   **Issue Fee:** If approved, you must pay an issue fee which varies based on your occupation (often CI$2,000 to CI$5,000).
*   **Annual Fee:** Once you hold PR, you must pay an annual fee equivalent to what your work permit fee would have been, until you obtain the Right to be Caymanian.
