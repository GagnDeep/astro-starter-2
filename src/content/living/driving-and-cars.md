Public transportation in the Cayman Islands consists of a network of privately owned minibuses. While functional for basic transit along major routes, most expats find that owning a car is essential for daily life.

## Rules of the Road

*   **Left-Side Driving:** Driving is on the left side of the road (British style).
*   **Roundabouts:** The island uses a vast network of roundabouts instead of traffic lights. Yield to traffic already on the roundabout (coming from your right).
*   **Speed Limits:** Limits are strict and enforced. The maximum speed on the island is 50 mph, but most residential and commercial areas are 25-30 mph.
*   **Drinking and Driving:** The legal blood alcohol limit is 0.07%, slightly lower than the US and UK (0.08%). Enforcement is rigorous.

## Converting Your Driver's License

Expats cannot drive indefinitely on their home country's license.
*   **The Exemption Period:** You can drive on your valid foreign license or an International Driving Permit for up to **6 months** after your arrival.
*   **Converting:** Before the 6 months expire, you must apply for a Cayman Islands Driver's License at the Department of Vehicle and Drivers' Licensing (DVDL).
*   **The Process:** If your license is from a recognized country (e.g., US, UK, Canada, Australia, Jamaica), you do not need to take a driving test. You simply pass a written theory test (which focuses heavily on roundabout rules), pay the fee, and exchange your license.

## Buying a Car: Import vs. Local Purchase

Cars in the Cayman Islands are expensive due to the massive import duties (29.5% for standard vehicles, up to 42% for luxury vehicles) levied on the CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) value.

### Option 1: Buying Locally
*   **Dealerships:** There are several authorized dealerships (e.g., GT Automotive, Vampt Motors) selling new Hondas, Toyotas, Fords, and luxury brands. The sticker price includes the duty.
*   **The Used Market:** The second-hand market is driven by expats leaving the island (often selling via the "EcayTrade" website). Used Japanese Domestic Market (JDM) imports—like the Honda Fit or Toyota Vitz—are the most common and practical vehicles on the island, generally costing between CI$6,000 and CI$10,000 used.

### Option 2: Importing Yourself
Some expats choose to buy a car in Miami or Japan and arrange shipping and customs clearance themselves.
*   **The Catch:** You must pay the 29.5%+ duty on the vehicle's value *plus* the cost of shipping. Furthermore, the Customs department uses an official valuation book (like Kelley Blue Book); they will assess the duty based on the *book value* of the car, even if you managed to buy it for a much lower price from a friend.
*   **Environmental Fee:** The government recently introduced an environmental disposal fee for older vehicles being imported.

## Insurance and Registration

*   **Insurance:** Third-party liability insurance is mandatory by law. Comprehensive insurance is required if you are financing the car through a local bank.
*   **Inspection and Licensing:** Every vehicle must pass an annual roadworthiness inspection at the DVDL before its registration (license plates/coupon) can be renewed.
