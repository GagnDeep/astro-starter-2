## The Raw Truth About the Home Office Write-Off

Forget what TikTok accountants tell you. The IRS does **not** allow you to write off your entire $3,000/month Brooklyn loft because you answered three client emails from your couch.

However, if you have a space used **regularly and exclusively** for business, you are sitting on one of the best tax write-offs available to self-employed freelancers.

### Method 1: The Simplified Method (Zero Paperwork Headache)
The IRS lets you deduct **$5.00 per square foot** of dedicated workspace up to a maximum of 300 square feet.
- **Maximum deduction:** $1,500 per tax year ($5.00 × 300 sq ft).
- **Paperwork needed:** Just measure the room dimensions. No utility bills or rent receipts required.

### Method 2: The Actual Expense Method (Big Savings for High Rent)
If your rent or mortgage is high, calculating actual expenses yields a much bigger tax shield.
- **Example:** You rent a 1,000 sq ft apartment for $2,500/month ($30,000/year). Your dedicated home office room is 200 sq ft (20% of total square footage).
- **Deductible Rent:** 20% of $30,000 = **$6,000**.
- **Deductible Utilities:** If your electricity and internet total $3,000/year, you deduct 20% = **$600**.
- **Total Write-Off:** **$6,600** on Form 8829.

> **CPA Warning Notice:** The "Exclusive Use" rule is strictly enforced by the IRS. A bedroom corner with a desk used exclusively for work counts. A desk that doubles as a gaming rig or dining table does not qualify under official IRS Publication 587 rules.
