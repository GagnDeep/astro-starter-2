## The Massive Health Insurance Tax Shield for Solo Workers

One of the biggest financial shocks when leaving a W-2 job is paying for health insurance out-of-pocket. Fortunately, Internal Revenue Code § 162(l) provides a powerful tax adjustment: **The Self-Employed Health Insurance Deduction**.

### Above-the-Line Deduction Benefit
Unlike standard business deductions on Schedule C, health insurance is deducted as an **above-the-line adjustment to income** on Form 1040 Schedule 1, Line 17.

This means you get the full tax benefit **even if you take the Standard Deduction** ($15,000 Single / $30,000 MFJ) on your tax return!

### Key Eligibility Restriction
You **cannot** take this deduction for any month in which you were eligible to participate in an employer-subsidized health plan maintained by your employer or your spouse's employer.

- **Example:** You pay $450/month ($5,400/year) for individual health coverage on Healthcare.gov. You have $35,000 in net freelance income. You deduct the full **$5,400** on Schedule 1 Line 17, lowering your AGI directly.
